## Findings

### High — AP07 is routed to per-use rights and copy lifecycle, while its shared disclosure composition has no task-level owner or witness

**Class:** new class in this review: cumulative disclosure accounting is distinct from per-use permission and paid-provider spend. Relevant patterns: P01/P02, P05, P14, P31, P37, P38. **Capability state:** `bridge_missing` and `semantic_test_missing`; the source-defined composition owner and its consumer bridge are not assigned in the machine task acceptance.

**Source and route:** `external-foundations/acquisition/05-implementation-and-review.md#AP07` requires overlapping releases, aliases, and recipients to use one source-defined disclosure composition; unknown overlap cannot be treated as disjoint; concurrent reserve/commit must be atomic; timeout with an unknown disclosure cannot return allowance; identical-output replay to the same audience is distinct from a fresh draw or new audience. `acquisition/03-storage-and-reuse.md#Совокупное-раскрытие` identifies the protected unit, overlap/lineage, recipient scope, query/output rule, and shared accounting boundary. The trace row `inventory/r9-obligations.json#AP07` routes to `LIF-05` and `CAP-06`; `workstreams/02-lifecycle.md` also assigns the scenario to LIF-05.

**Task-field gap:** `tasks.json#LIF-05.deliverable/positive/adversarial/removal` cover rights decisions, write fences, deletion placements, restore quarantine, and post-backup rights. None names a shared disclosure counter, source/custodian protected unit, release-overlap relation, concurrent reserve/commit, uncertain-output hold, or same-output replay rule. `tasks.json#CAP-03` also lists AP07 in `source_ids`, but its task fields cover fresh per-use purpose/audience rights decisions rather than composition across requests. `tasks.json#CAP-06.deliverable/positive/adversarial/removal` binds one remote request to roots, executable, recipient, and idempotency, but its positive and negative tests are per-request permission/receipt checks. Its `requires_other_mechanisms` mentions purpose-specific disclosure permission, not composition across requests. `tasks.json#OPS-07` accounts for monetary provider charges, which does not establish confidentiality composition. `CNT-03`'s no-query-only-raw-data surface rule protects storage of query-only data; it does not account for aggregate releases.

**Divergent execution:** two individually permitted aggregates over overlapping populations, issued through aliases or separate runs, each pass the current per-use grant and recipient checks. Their difference reveals an individual's value. Concurrent queries can both pass before either updates a shared allowance; a timeout after an unobserved disclosure can also free capacity for another query. The current task fields can accept the individual permissions and provider receipts while this source-level disclosure property fails.

**Fix:** add the source/custodian-defined protected unit, overlap/lineage, recipient/cooperation scope, and composition rule to the selected source-use contract. Assign the shared accounting transition to an existing canonical owner at the CAP-06/LIF-05 boundary; require atomic reserve/commit or retain an indeterminate reservation after uncertain output. Add a fresh-consumer probe for two overlapping concurrent releases, a lost response, exact same-output replay, and a new recipient/fresh draw. If the chosen profile does not support this, record a formal AP07 non-claim and refuse the affected output path.

### High when A2/web or LLM discovery is claimed — AP05 checks transport destination but does not test outbound query privacy

**Class:** new class in this review: disclosure of the outbound search request is distinct from AP07's composition of returned outputs. Relevant patterns: P05/P15, P37, P38. **Capability state:** `semantic_test_missing` for query disclosure; `bridge_missing` until query-purpose/recipient policy is bound to the actual search request.

**Source and route:** `external-foundations/acquisition/05-implementation-and-review.md#AP05` separately requires that page instructions cannot alter tools/rights and that source-domain query privacy be checked. `acquisition/01-acquisition-routes.md#Web-и-LLM` treats fetched text as data and says query/result retention depends on sensitivity and rights. The trace row `inventory/r9-obligations.json#AP05` routes to `CAP-01` and `CAP-02`. `workstreams/02-lifecycle.md` acknowledges query privacy as CAP-owned when activated.

**Task-field gap:** `tasks.json#CAP-01.adversarial` covers injected URLs and candidate/no-hit refusal; `tasks.json#CAP-02` covers redirect, DNS/socket/proxy, private-IP, credential-hop, and representation checks. Neither `positive`, `adversarial`, `removal`, nor `surface` binds the outbound query text to an authorized recipient, sensitivity/purpose, retention rule, or disclosure result. `tasks.json#CNT-03.surface` prevents query-only raw data from appearing in CAS/log/browser/provider temp; that is a returned-data storage boundary, not a check on what the external search provider receives. The prose crosswalk assigns ownership but does not supply that missing execution witness.

**Divergent execution:** an approved provider and allowed destination pass every hop/credential check while a search query contains a confidential institution, population, or policy question. The provider receives and may log it without a query-specific disclosure decision. This is possible even when the returned page is safely quarantined and candidate-only.

**Fix:** extend the CAP request/receipt with the selected query purpose, recipient/provider, sensitivity/retention decision, and allowed projection. Test that a page-injected instruction cannot expand tools or disclose protected context, and that an unauthorized provider/log sink receives no protected query. Keep the AP05 activation rule explicit: if web/LLM discovery is not in the claimed profile, record a non-claim; once claimed, require the query-privacy witness in the CAP acceptance.

### High when provision or issuer keys support admission — AP16 routes trust bootstrap to readiness/relation tasks, not an independent initial-trust proof

**Class:** new class in this review: trust-anchor bootstrap and compromised-root recovery are distinct from source-right checks and ordinary key rotation. Relevant patterns: P05, P32, P37, P38. **Capability state:** `bridge_missing` for binding an independently admitted trust provision into source admission; `verification_missing` for initial provision and compromised-root recovery witnesses.

**Source and route:** `external-foundations/acquisition/05-implementation-and-review.md#AP16` requires independent authorization for initial trust provision, successful authorized succession, rejection of self-consistent substitution/rollback/unauthorized recovery, and separation of historical signature validity from current use. `acquisition/06-operational-continuity.md#Trust-bootstrap-и-смена-ключей` says a hash proves byte identity, not authority to appoint an issuer, and calls for an authenticated initial provision plus an independent compromised-root recovery path. The trace row `inventory/r9-obligations.json#AP16` currently routes to `OPS-03`, `LIF-01`, and `DEB-09`.

**Task-field gap:** `tasks.json#OPS-03` tests effective profile, dependency readiness, and heartbeat; it does not establish who authorized the trust root. `tasks.json#LIF-01` resolves relation evidence for a selected use, and `tasks.json#DEB-09` handles institutional authority slots, but neither task binds initial trust-set provision to the deployment/release trust path. `LIF-08` lists AP16 in `source_ids` and has rollback/marker-removal checks, but its positive path is second-source compatibility; it does not positively establish an independently authorized initial provision. `OPS-11` names succession/compromise recovery, yet its positive is successor rotation and its adversarial field does not exercise recovery after compromise of the current root. `OPS-12`'s independent-root bundle check is limited to the conditional air-gapped profile. `OPS-01` carries a trust dossier and rejects an “untrusted root,” but the positive field only says named owners approve the selected source/use; it does not say how that initial root is authenticated independently of the provision package.

**Divergent execution:** a newly supplied provision contains a key and a self-hash signed by that same key. A profile/readiness check, content-bound source relation, and per-use grant can all be present while no prior operator/release trust path authorizes the key. Current tasks have no AP16 positive witness that distinguishes this from a legitimate initial provision, and no success-path witness for independent recovery after compromise.

**Fix:** correct AP16's `program_tasks` route to include the trust/bootstrap owners (at minimum LIF-08 plus OPS-01/OPS-11 when the selected profile relies on these keys). Add a task-level positive for an independently authorized initial provision bound to exact digest, scope, and trust-set generation; a negative for a self-signed replacement and old-root rollback; a separate authorized recovery after current-root compromise; and a fresh CAP-03/source consumer proving only the admitted trust generation supports current use. Keep an operator-selected root limited to a declared local candidate prototype, without promoting it to institutional competence.
