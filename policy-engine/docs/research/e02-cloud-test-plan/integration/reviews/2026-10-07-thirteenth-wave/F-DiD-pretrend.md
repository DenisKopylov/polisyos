## What the change measures

The `clean_rollout` fixture has 6 units (3 treated, 3 control), 4 periods, treatment time 2, and therefore 2 pre- plus 2 post-periods. The unchanged native `StandardDifferenceInDifferences` estimator returns ATT `1.997217038863679` against fixture truth 2.0. An independent four-group/time-means calculation returns `1.9972170388636759` (difference `3.1e-15`). The estimator's exact two-period pretrend diagnostic is explicitly `not_testable`: no statistic or p-value, `passed=False`, reason `insufficient_pre_periods`, and no identification authority. With two points the fitted intercept-plus-time slope has no residual degrees of freedom; this benchmark must not turn non-rejection or a boolean into evidence of parallel trends.

The old checker used diagnostic-list position and a `passed` bit as a proxy for pretrend testability. That proxy rejected an otherwise correct positive ATT solely because a two-pre-period test correctly was not testable. The new checker selects the named diagnostic and verifies its semantic disposition. The independent probe executes the actual runner and independently computes ATT, then applies ten falsifiers: fabricated `passed=True`, statistic, p-value, wrong/missing status or reason, asserted/missing authority field, missing diagnostic, and ATT drift. All ten are refused. This is a bounded checker/known-DGP discriminator, not an independent statistical test of trends or a general proof of DiD identification.

The consumer is the benchmark harness: `BenchmarkHarness._run_case` turns this checker's result into `CaseResult.verdict`; the reporting layer carries that verdict and result payload into the benchmark JSON/aggregate rows. The earlier independent API probe directly called this single `BenchmarkCase` checker and did **not** exercise the complete three-row output. A subsequent G-native isolated-source smoke did exercise the three-case module; its pin and limits are recorded below. `build_harness()` registers exactly clean rollout, placebo null, and staggered adoption.
