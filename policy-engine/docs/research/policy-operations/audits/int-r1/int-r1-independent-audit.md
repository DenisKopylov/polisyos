# INT-R1 — Independent Adversarial Audit

## 1. Executive verdict

**Overall verdict: `GO_WITH_REVISIONS`.**

The audited deliverable establishes a defensible narrow result:

1. **Conditional impossibility:** while the admissible world class remains open under an
   observationally invisible decisive-obligation extension, no finite trace available to
   PolicyOS can soundly and positively certify global obligation completeness.
2. **Relative positive result:** PolicyOS can prove/check mechanical inclusion and validation
   relative to a declared, content-bound basis and obligation language, under explicit semantic,
   governance, independence, and currentness assumptions.
3. **Honest public consequence:** `P(false promotion | maintained assumptions) ≤ δ` may be
   rendered only as risk control relative to the declared obligation set and maintained
   assumptions; it is not a bound on the probability that an applicable obligation was omitted.
4. **Lifecycle consequence:** a later missed obligation or validator defect leaves historical
   arithmetic inspectable but suspends current reliance and requires append-only challenge,
   revalidation, supersession/reissue, and public reaction by the canonical claim owner.

No blocking circularity, self-scoring benchmark, parallel authority lattice, hidden capability
claim, or scope violation was found. The audited branch adds six Markdown research files only.
It explicitly says the benchmark was not implemented or run and that the current capability is
`semantic_test_missing`.

The result cannot be consolidated verbatim. Six material corrections are required:

- the relative theorem decomposes/relocates the maintained assumptions; it does not discharge
  compiler semantic completeness or validator soundness;
- theorem premises must be separated from governed evidence/admissibility criteria;
- actual independence is not constructed, so the pinned repository cannot issue
  `bounded_complete`;
- the impossibility premise must be discharged or retained per actual scope rather than presumed
  universally;
- the decisive-obligation deletion fixture needs an obligation-instance/aggregation bridge to be
  executable against the current one-record-per-class N9 representation; and
- the categorical Rule-12 defect verdict against `PromotionObligationClass` must be narrowed to
  the enum's **use as a universal world boundary**, not its legitimate use as a governed versioned
  denominator vocabulary.

- [claim–evidence ledger](int-r1-claim-evidence-ledger.md);
- [anchor and citation verification](int-r1-anchor-and-citation-verification.md);
- [formal argument audit](int-r1-formal-argument-audit.md);

## 6. Pass C — impossibility result

The construction is valid:

- identical finite trace means a trace-only deterministic procedure receives identical input;
- a randomized procedure has the same conditional output distribution in both worlds;
- a positive global-completeness certificate is false in the world containing unseen decisive
  `o*`; and
- uniform refusal does not positively certify the closed world.

The theorem is therefore a valid **conditional indistinguishability lemma**. The report is honest
that a narrow competent register or closure rule can rule out the extension world. It does not
claim mathematical impossibility after such a premise is established.

The unresolved issue is application. INT-R1 did not classify actual obligation domains or scopes
by whether a competent exhaustive register exists. The consolidated protocol must require an
explicit, evidenced per-scope disposition rather than treating “open world” as a universal fact.

The five stated consequences are valid with this condition. Search quantity, TTL, review, enum
equality, and randomization do not create identification where the same trace remains compatible
with both worlds.

**Pass-C disposition:** result stands, with material scope clarification.

## 7. Pass D — relative theorem and regress

### 7.1 What the theorem proves

The conclusion—every obligation derivable from basis `B` under declared language/compiler `v`
is included and checked—follows under the stated semantic assumptions. This is a legitimate
relative inclusion result.

### 7.2 What it assumes

Premise 4 assumes the compiler is sound and complete relative to the basis semantics. Premise 6
assumes validators are sound relative to their predicates/domains. These are not consequences of
basis hashing, traversal, or a typed envelope. They are the semantic assumptions whose adequacy
is at issue.

Compared with target-spec A4, INT-R1 adds useful decomposition:

- generic traversal can be made complete-by-construction over an owned basis;
- source-to-obligation identities can be content-bound;
- mutation and independent reperformance can falsify named fault classes;
- known defeaters and currentness can be governed; and
- the open remainder can remain explicit.

That improves checkability. It does not prove semantic completeness of the compiler or validator.
The report's result should therefore be consolidated as a **conditional relative-inclusion
theorem plus a governed admissibility protocol**, not as discharge of `obligation_completeness`
or `validator_soundness`.

### 7.3 Did the regress stop?

It stopped correctly for the mechanical properties PolicyOS owns:

- all fields/nested objects in the actual basis were traversed;
- all declared routes were exercised;
- all compiler outputs were content-bound; and
- future owned-schema additions cannot silently evade a generic traversal with genuine typed
  exemptions.

It did not—and cannot by that move—stop the external-world adequacy question. Basis selection and
semantic validator soundness remain institutional/empirical assumptions with evidence,
challenge, and fail-closed consequences. That is not a failed result so long as it remains
visible.

### 7.4 Independence

The report correctly says a second function name is not independence and enumerates common-mode
dimensions. The proposed records make dependencies checkable. No actual independent checker,
source-to-obligation oracle, scorer, or governance producer is constructed. S0-GAP-02 remains
pending. Therefore the pinned repository cannot truthfully issue `bounded_complete`.

**Pass-D disposition:** accepted only with material theorem/protocol separation and explicit
current-capability refusal.

## 8. Pass E — circularity, self-attestation, and oracle independence

The audit specifically searched for the OPS-R15 failure shape: expected results produced by code

The INT-R1 benchmark requires:

- an immutable source corpus;
- an independently authored source-to-obligation oracle;

a P29 counterexample and requires independent source-level reperformance, governance, and

**Pass-E disposition:** cleared; preserve the oracle-independence and non-passage language.

## 9. Pass F — `bounded_complete` laundering and the one lattice

The design passes the authority-boundary audit:

- `bounded_complete` means only relative coverage for the exact basis/language/scope/cutoff;
- it provides no substantive obligation satisfaction and never sets `promoted`;
- `known_incomplete` and `open_world_unresolved` map to existing `failed`, `unknown`, or
  `scope_insufficient` outcomes depending on the witness;
- candidate-band work may continue only with the unknown carried forward;
- protected action and public claim fail closed for the affected scope;
- stale, contradictory, revoked, expired, or suspended decisive evidence cannot become a pass;
- public surfaces cannot mint or upgrade authority; and
- no coverage passage claims legal compliance or competence.

One notation should be tightened. `NO_COVERAGE_BLOCKER` is safe only as pseudocode meaning the
absence of an additional coverage blocker. If persisted/rendered/ordered, it would become a
parallel status. Consolidation must prohibit that use.

**Pass-F disposition:** cleared with one minor anti-laundering clarification.

## 10. Pass G — `PromotionObligationClass` and Rule 12

The report correctly proves that the enum is load-bearing:

- the confidence ledger partitions it exactly;
- N9 emits/validates an exact ordered denominator;
- mismatch enters refusal reasons; and
- promotion is recomputed from those reasons.

The report also correctly says exact equality to the enum does not prove all world obligations
were represented.

The categorical conclusion that the enum is therefore a Rule-12 capability-gating defect is too
strong. Rule 12 expressly permits governed vocabularies, schemas, statuses, ports, and rule
versions. A versioned denominator may legitimately gate a receipt **relative to that declared
version**. Its defect arises when it is represented as the universal world obligation set or
prevents actual source-derived obligation growth without a governed amendment/extension path.

No actual unrepresentable obligation was demonstrated, and research may not authorize a change to
the live waist. Consolidation should retain the report's own use-sensitive table and remove the
categorical defect sentence.

**Pass-G disposition:** material narrowing required; no authorization to edit the enum.

The report's definition of red is strong enough. A witnessed assumption breach must produce:

- `protected_action_allowed = false`;
- `current_public_claim_allowed = false`;
- existing-lattice failure/unknown/scope-insufficient effect;

- revalidation/reissue; and
- stable historical replay of the old arithmetic/record.

A red auxiliary report with still-green promotion/public surface is explicitly a benchmark
failure.

### 11.3 OM-01 executability defect

The current PDC record carries one `obligation_class`; the current N9 compiler constructs one
record per 15-member enum value and validates exact tuple equality. The proposed OM-01 fixture
requires two obligation **instances** in the same class, deletion of one, and preservation of the
class row.

That fixture is conceptually correct against a future source-derived instance layer but is not
currently implementable without specifying:

- the pre-aggregation instance collection;
- stable instance identity;
- instance-to-class aggregation semantics; and
- the independent comparison point.

This does not refute the benchmark principle. It means the deliverable has not met its own “build
without asking a question” standard for OM-01.

## 14. Why the verdict is not `NO_GO`

A `NO_GO` would be warranted if the central result depended on an unstated universal closure
claim, if the relative theorem were presented as world completeness, if independence were
self-attested as achieved, or if benchmark passage were claimed without an independent oracle.
The report avoids those failures:

- it calls the impossibility theorem premise-relative;
- it explicitly denies `C_v(B)=U(W)`;
- it carries unknown remainder and limitations;
- it declares independence unresolved when common-mode;
- it blocks self-oracles;
- it says no benchmark was run; and
- it maps evidence assessments into existing fail-closed statuses.

The material findings narrow a valid research result; they do not overturn it.

## 16. Final audit statement

The INT-R1 deliverable is fit to enter consolidation as an **`accepted_narrow_scope` research
result with mandatory revisions**. It does not prove open-world completeness. It does not prove
its compiler or validators sound. It does provide a rigorous boundary around what may be proved,
a credible design for making the remaining premises inspectable and challengeable, and a
fail-closed public/lifecycle protocol that avoids converting a declared unknown into false
certainty.

That narrow result is load-bearing and worth preserving. Its qualifications are equally
load-bearing and must survive consolidation word for word in substance.
