## 2. Pass VII — ratified-kernel conformance

### 2.1 `INT-K02` — every `delta` retains its declared set, assumptions and rider

**Ratified rule.** A bare `delta` is not a smaller statement; it is an incomplete and different
statement that cannot be projected as authority.

**INT-R8 treatment.** The rule is applied categorically in:

- the minimum retained set;
- the loss-boundary calibration anchor;
- `INT-R8-F02`;
- property invariants; and
- the main report's ratified-constraint section.

Removing the obligation set, maintained assumptions or relative-basis rider always yields
`blocked_material_omission`. No editor override, full-record pointer, readability exception or
materiality score is permitted.

**Verdict:** conforms strongly.

### 2.2 `INT-K06` — custody claim without a risk number

**Ratified rule.** A bounded falsifiable claim may cover prospectivity, firstness,
substitutions, chronology, adjudication, deviations, negative terminals, publication and
correction while carrying no statistical family number.

**INT-R8 treatment.** Prefix discipline is stated as a pass/fail claim over:

- prospectively evaluated actual transcript prefixes;
- reproducible membership, chronology, heads, assumptions and verifier versions;
- append-only correction/supersession; and
- no post-hoc history narrowing.

The claim has direct falsifiers: local-only checking, missing procedural step, deleted transcript
member and an invented number. It is therefore falsifiable as a research contract.

The repository cannot currently emit or verify that claim. INT-R8 says so. Binding here means
“a protected publication may rely on the claim only after the contract is independently
implemented and verified,” not “the research file itself grants authority.”

**Verdict:** conforms semantically; capability remains absent.

### 2.3 `INT-K08` — negative completion remains visible

**Ratified rule.** Refusal, void, dispute, terminal no-attempt and exhaustion are completed
outcomes. Compression cannot convert absence of success into permission to hide them.

**INT-R8 treatment.** Every named terminal is in the mandatory set, loss boundary and F03
mutation family. Hidden terminal is always blocked. The report does not say the terminal is
substantively correct merely because it is retained.

**Verdict:** conforms strongly.

### 2.4 `INT-K04` — numerical family claims require prospective local bounds and canonical custody

INT-R8 issues no numerical family claim. It expressly refuses epsilon, percentages, risk scores
and remaining-budget language. Prefix discipline is a Boolean per-prefix predicate rather than a
sum of local risks.

Pass III found one overstatement: deterministic channels do not rule out every quantitative
information-leakage framework. After that sentence is narrowed, the current no-number result
still conforms because no locally valid leakage quantity or canonical adaptive accountant exists
in the repository.

**Verdict:** conforms after the wording correction required by `INT-R8-III-002`.

### 2.5 `INT-K07` — adaptivity

INT-R8 evaluates the actual next release together with actual prior history. The release can be
selected after earlier outcomes; no numerical validity claim is transferred from a preselected
mechanism. For a fixed versioned Boolean rule family, the prefix induction handles this
adaptivity.

A heuristic or estimated reconstruction check would require a separate validity argument. That
is the required qualification in `INT-R8-III-004`.

**Verdict:** conforms for exact/proved-conservative Boolean checks.

### 2.6 `S0-K07` — projection cannot mint authority

The receipt:

- remains `projection_only`;
- has empty `authoritative_for`;
- carries existing denied uses;
- cannot turn `lossy_but_safe` into approval, publication, closeout or claim authority; and
- has a dedicated authority-laundering falsifier F22.

Public export remains a projection and Atlas remains a renderer. No semantic claim is promoted
merely because it was summarized or signed.

**Verdict:** conforms.

### 2.7 `INT-K05` — one owner/no second confidence ledger

INT-R8 explicitly refuses:

- a parent confidence scope;
- a second confidence/risk ledger;
- a receipt-owned authority lattice; and
- frontend-local materiality authority.

`lossy_but_safe` and `blocked_material_omission` are defined as verifier outcomes feeding an
existing gate, not a global status lattice. The research does not appoint the future transcript
owner.

The unresolved reason/materiality registry boundary in `INT-R8-V-003` must be repaired to keep
that reuse promise real, but no second ledger is actually created by the audited text.

**Verdict:** conforms with a duplication-risk gap.

## 4. Pass VIII — ownership boundary

### 4.1 What INT-R8 legitimately owns

INT-R8 owns the **content relation** between a full source record, a projected/condensed public
object and the accumulated disclosure transcript. It may require proof inputs for:

- the source revision;
- audience and surface;
- retained semantic-item set;
- omitted item classes, affected claims and safe canonical reasons;
- loss verdict and rule version;
- predecessor/current transcript head;
- current/superseded relation; and
- the canonical transformed public object.

It may also require that proof material not itself reveal dropped content through a raw hash,
identifier, commitment, proof size or join key.

### 4.2 What INT-R7 legitimately owns

INT-R7 owns proof and lifecycle construction, including decisions about:

- signature and commitment algorithms;
- canonicalization and proof format;
- key issuance, rotation, revocation and succession;
- transparency/anti-equivocation mechanics;
- timestamp and archival verification;
- offline verification;
- proof survival and successor relations across permitted transformations; and
- long-term verification policy.

INT-R8 does not choose any of these.

### 4.3 Boundary-crossing hunt

Across 6/6 INT-R8 files, no passage:

- selects a signature algorithm;
- fixes a key type, key lifetime, rotation or revocation policy;
- appoints a signer, custodian, witness or archive;
- chooses a transparency-log construction;
- defines offline verifier mechanics;
- fixes a proof wire schema; or
- claims that a signature by itself proves semantic truth.

The phrase “proof survives permitted redaction” is used as an **interface requirement**, not as a
claim that arbitrary byte deletion leaves an ordinary signature valid. The sharper formulation
in §13 is correct: redaction is a well-defined transformation of the proof-bound object, the
transformed public object is verifiable under INT-R7's construction, and proof material does not
become an oracle for the removed value.

### 4.4 What the declared seam already gets right

INT-R8 requires binding of the source, audience, retained set, omission classes/reasons, verdict,
rule version, transcript head and currentness. It also states:

- content selection remains with INT-R8;
- cryptographic construction remains with INT-R7;
- protected raw hashes/identifiers are forbidden as a shortcut; and
- current and historical meaning are separate.

This is compatible with `INT-R7-VIII-001`, which commended the proof/content division.

### 4.5 Missing bindable inputs

The interface is not yet sufficient to prove the formal claim INT-R8 actually defines. A proof
that binds only `rule_version` and a retained set can be valid while the verifier silently
changes the model. The bindable input set must also identify or carry a typed disposition for:

1. **declared use set** against which semantic parity was evaluated;
2. **governed decision-predicate/materiality package** used for truth/scope/use comparisons;
3. **full-record semantic-inventory version** and its completeness disposition;
4. **record/consistency model** defining `R` and observational consistency;
5. **protected-predicate family** `F`/`q` checked for reconstruction;
6. **channel registry and release-family boundary** included in the transcript;
7. **audience coalition/delegation model** used for cross-view availability;
8. **auxiliary/background-information model** and its stated incompleteness;
9. **exact rendered/exported object identifiers** for page, print, deep link and machine export;
10. **verifier status for timeout, empty consistency set and abstraction mode**; and
11. **transcript-completeness disposition**, especially for uncontrolled external copies.

This list is semantic, not a proposed wire schema. INT-R7 may choose how to bind it.

### 4.6 Projection failure must not rewrite issuer authenticity

The parallel audit finding `INT-R7-VIII-003` remains load-bearing. INT-R7's audited aggregate
allowed `ProjectionRelationValid` to flow through statement completeness into issuance and
historical authenticity. An INT-R8 failure should instead mean:

- issuer-side original issuance may still be authentic;
- public projection faithfulness/loss safety is not established;
- current/public positive verification is blocked; and
- historical issuer facts are not rewritten.

INT-R8's interface should state this separation explicitly so a future proof implementation
cannot interpret `blocked_material_omission` as “the issuer never issued the source record.”
This is not adoption of INT-R7's proposed repair; it is a required semantic non-substitution rule
at the seam.

### 4.7 Current INT-R7 standing

`INT-R7-VIII-002` recorded that no delivered INT-R8 content contract existed at the time of the
INT-R7 audit. The present audited branch supplies such a research contract, so that factual
absence is superseded for research-integration purposes. It does not close:

- INT-R7's blocking `INT-R7-X-001` capability-label finding;
- INT-R7's material formal/fixture findings;
- implementation of either side; or
- the first-public-signature gate.

The seam remains a contract between two research artifacts under revision, not an executable
proof path.

## 6. Seam conclusion

The seam is structurally sound and construction-neutral. INT-R8 does not invade INT-R7's proof
or key lifecycle, and INT-R7 need not decide content materiality. The missing inputs are not a
request for a wire schema; they are the semantic parameters without which a signature could bind
a receipt while failing to bind the theorem the receipt claims to report.
