# INT-R9 — Proving the First Positive Governed Promotion Without Cherry-Picking

## Executive Finding

**Result: `accepted_narrow_scope`. Current execution readiness: `blocked`.**

PolicyOS can prospectively govern the *procedure* by which a first positive promotion
would be recognized, but it cannot prove the unqualified proposition “the first positive
was not cherry-picked.” The strongest defensible claim is narrower:

> The earliest qualifying attempt in a finite, prospectively committed sequence was
> evaluated under the named implementation revision, environment, case-selection rule,
> sealed primary and adjacent packages, existing owner gates, evaluator version,
> confidence-ledger scope, adjudicator identities, maintained assumptions, and protocol
> version; no prohibited post-result selection or case-specific implementation was found
> in the governed record.

That is a custody-and-procedure claim, not a theorem excluding undisclosed collusion,
fabricated records, every form of semantic contamination, or upstream topic-selection
bias. It does not establish population performance, legal compliance, institutional
competence, production readiness, or the existence of another promotable case.

The current repository cannot execute even that bounded protocol. All 13 real
proving-ground cases and both synthetic adjudications are public, answer-bearing
regression material; there is no sealed decisive case or adjacent unseen case. The
current reviewer identities are role placeholders rather than accountable independent
humans. S0-GAP-02, which owns the reusable oracle/evaluator custody architecture, remains
commissioned rather than delivered. INT-R1, which must provide the bounded obligation-set
declaration consumed by this protocol, is running in parallel. These are blocking
prerequisites, not details to be filled after a promising result appears.

The central disposition is deliberately costly: **`ua-msme-affordable-loans-2022` is
ineligible as the decisive first-promotion holdout.** It remains mandatory public
regression material. It is the only case against which the full composed loop has been
built and exercised, it appears in development evidence, its expected answer is committed
in the tree, and the N9 input carries a ua-msme governed-promotion reference by default.
Using it would satisfy “no selection after this run” only by ignoring the much larger
history of case-conditioned development. Excluding it means that the first governed
attempt may refuse, dispute, or never converge. That is the price of an honest first
claim.

The protocol therefore precommits a finite three-slot attempt sequence selected from a
larger independently authored and committed case-pair pool; seals every selected primary,
adjacent, and expectation package before first inspection; fixes criteria, order,
stopping, publication, and amendment rules; freezes implementation and environment before
each input reveal; requires named human adjudication; treats public regressions as visible
regressions rather than holdout evidence; and publishes refusal, void, dispute, and queue
exhaustion with the same durability as promotion. Three slots, a six-pair pool, and a
three-person panel are **engineering conveniences**, not theorems. A consolidation pass
may choose different finite numbers while preserving the properties.

## 1. Task And Project Fit

### 1.1 Exact question

The research question is:

> How does PolicyOS prove the first positive governed promotion without cherry-picking —
> and without promising that a positive result exists at all?

The decisive object is not a generic benchmark score. It is the first transition from a
candidate/shadow posture to a positive authority-bearing promotion. Every discretionary
choice made before that transition can affect its meaning: case, case order, criterion,
materiality interpretation, threshold reference, implementation revision, source cutoff,
run, retry, reviewer, exclusion, stopping, and public wording. A procedure authored after
any of those choices becomes result-bearing cannot recover prospectivity.

This is why the GY verification plan says that INT-R9 must be ratified before a promotion
candidate is inspected, while also defining verification success to include a real
positive **or** an honest grounded abstention/repair terminal rather than forcing useful
credit (`policy-engine/docs/plans/active/layer3-slices/GY-engine-subordination.md:2500-2575`).
The repository constitution likewise says B output remains shadow until A grounds it and
the promotion gate passes, prohibits optimization of `useful_design_rate`, requires one
status lattice, and bans capability-gating hard-code fallbacks
(`policy-engine/docs/system-design-decisions/universal-policy-design-system-vision-and-organizing-rules.md:145-230`).

### 1.2 Why research must precede implementation

The false production claim prevented here is:

> “PolicyOS produced its first governed positive result, therefore the system has shown
> that it can promote a real policy design honestly.”

Without a prospective protocol, that sentence can hide at least six nonrecoverable
selection effects:

1. the case was chosen because it was already tractable;
2. the system was shaped around that case;
3. visible expected answers guided binding, stopping, or interpretation;
4. failed candidates or runs disappeared;
5. reviewers or criteria were selected after result-bearing information was known;
6. public wording generalized beyond the one revision, environment, case, evaluator, and
   maintained-assumption set actually tested.

No later audit can reconstruct counterfactual choices that were never logged. Research is
therefore not a ceremonial preface to code. It defines which facts must exist before any
result can be admissible at all.

### 1.3 Fit with the ratified identity and custody boundary

The ratified identity decision makes PolicyOS the epistemic custodian of policy
justification, not the administrator, adjudicator, service operator, payment operator, or
source of external institutional authority
(`policy-engine/docs/system-design-decisions/policyos-identity-and-custody-boundary.md:35-220`).
INT-R9 fits only if it governs the honesty of PolicyOS's own promotion claim and integrates,
rather than appropriates, external adjudication and case facts.

| Four-way verdict | INT-R9 disposition |
| --- | --- |
| **OWN** | Custody of the preregistration, attempt order, implementation freeze, owner receipts, adjudication, deviations, dispute, promotion/refusal/void outcome, bounded public wording, and later correction of PolicyOS's own promotion claim. |
| **INTEGRATE** | Externally authored case facts and expectations; named human expertise and conflict disclosures; S0-GAP-02 commitment/access/challenge machinery; INT-R1 obligation-set declaration; canonical GY-N9/N11/N12 owner outputs; external institutional/delegation evidence. |
| **OBSERVE** | Funder, employer, reporting-line, organizational incentive, adjudicator availability, criticism, challenge, and correction signals that may change admissibility or public currentness but are not PolicyOS-created facts. |
| **OUT_OF_SCOPE** | Policy administration or implementation; legal-effect adjudication; certification of institutional competence; guarantee of representativeness; guarantee that a positive case exists; replacement of external source, court, regulator, evaluator, or public-record authority. |

### 1.4 What this result is and is not

The research result is an **accepted narrow governance protocol**. It is not an operational
capability claim. The protocol is executable in the sense that it specifies the inputs,
sequence, owners, records, clocks, stopping, failures, and public meanings needed for an
attempt. Execution is blocked until the repository has fresh independently held cases,
identified humans, an approved S0-GAP-02 mechanism, and an INT-R1 declaration.

A negative terminal is a completed protocol result. `refused`, `void`, `disputed`,
`terminal_no_attempt`, and `exhausted_without_promotion` must be as publishable and
reviewable as `promoted`. The protocol does not contain a success quota, a deadline by
which a positive must exist, or an exception allowing a “nearly passing” case to be
substituted.

### 2.6 The ua-msme dilemma: chosen horn and cost

**Chosen horn:** exclude ua-msme from the decisive primary and adjacent holdout roles.

The contrary choice would treat “the protocol did not select the case after seeing this
specific final run” as equivalent to “the case did not shape the system.” The repository
establishes the opposite. ua-msme is the only integrated-depth case; it appears in
universality-development evidence; its answer is public; and N9 names it in the current
input default. A fresh run cannot erase those facts.

**Cost:** the first governed attempt must occur on a newly authored case that has never
completed the loop. It may refuse or expose a missing bridge. It may reveal that the
system's current generality is much weaker than ua-msme suggested. The protocol accepts
that outcome and prohibits reverting to ua-msme for a more favorable headline.

**Reopening evidence:** only a custody and causal-isolation proof showing that prior
ua-msme work could not affect any tested predicate, binding, configuration, source choice,
criteria interpretation, or implementation behavior would justify reconsideration. A new
hidden label alone is insufficient because it does not undo case-selection or
implementation contamination. Nothing in the inspected tree supports reopening.

## 3. External Research Baseline

### 3.1 Transfer rule

PolicyOS's first promotion is **not** a randomized trial, a sample mean, or a conventional
hypothesis test. It has `n = 1` as a historical event, and the controlled risk is a false
authority claim under declared assumptions, not merely a false rejection of a statistical
null. External fields therefore supply mechanisms and warnings, not wholesale proof.

| External field | What transfers | What does not transfer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adaptive data analysis and reusable holdouts | Repeated access makes a holdout part of development; access/feedback must be limited and accounted for; one-time fresh cases are valuable. | Dwork et al.'s distributional and privacy-based generalization theorems do not establish correctness of a PolicyOS authority claim or apply automatically to non-i.i.d. policy cases. |
| Trial registration, SPIRIT, AEA registries | Prospective time, version history, explicit amendments, declared outcomes/criteria, and public deviations reduce invisible degrees of freedom. | Registry existence alone is not sealing or independent review; AEA permits registration at any study phase, so its registry rule is weaker than INT-R9's required prospectivity. |
| Registered Reports | Plan evaluation before outcomes; result-independent acceptance/publication; outcome-neutral quality checks and positive controls. | Editorial acceptance is not authority admission, and a methods-compliant null/negative study is not analogous to a positive promotion. |
| Researcher degrees of freedom | Honest actors can create favorable paths through ambiguous choices without deliberate fraud; bind case, criteria, exclusions, run, stopping, and reporting, not only the headline threshold. | The published false-positive simulations do not numerically quantify PolicyOS's false-promotion probability. |
| Group-sequential and anytime-valid methods | Attempt order and stopping must be predeclared; cumulative risk accounting must survive failures and repeated looks. | Pocock or O'Brien–Fleming boundaries are not a new PolicyOS allocation rule. The repository's admitted confidence-ledger profile remains authoritative. |
| Single-pivotal-trial regulatory practice | A single decisive result, where permitted, is paired with independent confirmatory evidence, context, quality, and bounded claims rather than treated as self-authenticating. | FDA guidance is product-specific, legally situated, and nonbinding in draft form; it does not authorize PolicyOS or make one case representative. |
| Benchmark contamination and rebuilt test sets | Public, repeatedly optimized benchmarks require fresh one-time tests; literal string scans miss semantic contamination; matched fresh cases can expose overfit. | A rebuilt test set may differ in difficulty/distribution; a performance drop does not identify adaptivity alone, and an adjacent policy case is not a random population sample. |
| Inter-rater reliability | Preserve raw votes and disagreement; use Cohen's κ or Krippendorff's α as diagnostics where their assumptions fit; calibrate the guide before sealing. | Agreement is not correctness, independence, authority, or substantive validity. No universal coefficient threshold is imported here. |
| Publication bias/file drawer | Result-independent publication and organizational rewards reduce the pressure to hide null/negative outcomes. | Publication rules cannot prove the absence of informal retaliation or career incentives. |
| Cryptographic commitment and time-stamping | Binding, hiding, canonical serialization, signatures, and independently verifiable proof-of-existence help establish what was fixed and when. | A plain hash is not secrecy; cryptography does not establish semantic correctness, human independence, or absence of side channels. |

### 3.2 Adaptive reuse: why the public corpus is not a holdout

Dwork et al. formalize the problem of analyses chosen adaptively after seeing holdout
feedback and provide mechanisms for valid reuse in a statistical setting
([Science 2015, DOI 10.1126/science.aaa9375](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaa9375);
[STOC 2015, DOI 10.1145/2746539.2746580](https://doi.org/10.1145/2746539.2746580)).
The transferable conclusion is structural: exposure changes the epistemic role of a test
set. The current outcome corpus has supplied inputs, expected claims, labels, gold cards,
reviewer votes, and development evidence; it is therefore public regression and
calibration material. Calling it “sealed” would be false even if access were restricted
tomorrow.

The theorem does **not** transfer directly. Policy cases are heterogeneous, the decisive
claim is semantic and authority-bearing, and the repository is not estimating expectations
over an identified sampling distribution. INT-R9 therefore uses access separation and a
fresh one-time case to prevent known adaptation, while making no Dwork-style population
generalization claim.

### 3.3 Registration, amendments, and their failure modes

ICMJE requires trial registration at or before first participant enrollment and explicitly
states that retrospective registration does not serve the anti-selective-publication and
anti-selective-reporting purposes of prospective registration
([ICMJE clinical-trial registration recommendations](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/publishing-and-editorial-issues/clinical-trial-registration.html)).
SPIRIT requires dated versions and plans for communicating important protocol modifications
([SPIRIT 2013 explanation, BMJ 346:e7586](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.e7586)).
The AEA RCT Registry preserves version history and permits amendments, but allows
registration during any phase while encouraging early registration
([AEA RCT Registry policy](https://www.aeaweb.org/journals/policies/rct-registry)).

INT-R9 takes the strict part: the governing version must be independently visible before
any outcome-relevant inspection; amendments remain versioned; and post-inspection changes
cannot rescore the old run. It rejects the weak part: “registered before publication” is
not enough.

Preregistration is not a magic compliance field. Claesen et al. compared 27 early
preregistered studies with their plans: only two had no deviations, one disclosed all
deviations, and nine disclosed none
([Royal Society Open Science 2021, DOI 10.1098/rsos.211037](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.211037)).
That result does not estimate an INT-R9 failure probability, but it establishes that
deviations are normal enough to require a first-class deviation record rather than a
fiction of perfect adherence.

### 3.4 Registered Reports and outcome-neutral acceptance

Registered Reports move peer review and in-principle publication acceptance before results
and retain Stage-2 checks for adherence, quality controls, and justified conclusions
([Scientific Reports policy](https://www.nature.com/srep/journal-policies/registered-reports)).
This is the closest incentive design for making a null result survivable: assess the plan
and execution rather than the sign of the result.

Scheel, Schijen, and Lakens found positive results for 96% of sampled standard reports and
44% of Registered Reports, while explicitly discussing alternative explanations rather
than treating the difference as a randomized causal estimate
([Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science 2021, DOI
10.1177/25152459211007467](https://doi.org/10.1177/25152459211007467)).
INT-R9 transfers result-independent publication and outcome-neutral controls. It does not
infer that a lower positive rate is automatically better or set a target promotion rate.

### 3.5 Researcher degrees of freedom

Simmons, Nelson, and Simonsohn show how flexible collection, analysis, and reporting choices
can inflate false positives and emphasize that self-serving interpretation can occur
without an intent to deceive
([Psychological Science 2011, DOI 10.1177/0956797611417632](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611417632)).
Gelman and Loken describe the “garden of forking paths”: data-dependent analytical choices
create uncounted alternatives even without a fishing expedition
([American Scientist 2014, DOI 10.1511/2014.111.460](https://doi.org/10.1511/2014.111.460)).

The direct INT-R9 analogue is that binding only the case name is decorative. Criteria,
materiality, source cutoff, exclusions, retries, implementation revision, reviewer,
stopping, and public scope must also be fixed or governed before results.

### 3.6 Sequential attempts and the existing risk ledger

Pocock and O'Brien–Fleming show that repeated looks and early stopping require a design that
controls the aggregate procedure rather than treating every look as a fresh test
([Pocock 1977, DOI 10.1093/biomet/64.2.191](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/64.2.191);
[O'Brien & Fleming 1979, DOI 10.2307/2530245](https://doi.org/10.2307/2530245)).
Modern confidence-sequence work provides time-uniform alternatives under specified
assumptions
([Howard et al. 2021, DOI 10.1214/20-AOS1991](https://doi.org/10.1214/20-AOS1991)).

INT-R9 imports only the procedural lesson. The repository already has a predictable
Basel-square schedule and admitted owner/e-process profiles. The protocol must bind each
attempt to that ledger, preserve chronological ordinals, and prohibit budget resets or
parallel accounting. It does not derive a new statistical theorem.

### 3.7 One pivotal result plus corroboration

FDA's June 2026 revised draft says that, in context, one adequate and well-controlled
clinical investigation may be paired with confirmatory evidence to meet the substantial-
evidence standard; the document is a draft, not for implementation, and contains
nonbinding recommendations
([FDA June 2026 revised draft](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/demonstrating-substantial-evidence-effectiveness-human-drug-and-biological-products)).
The 2023 companion draft similarly discusses one investigation plus confirmatory evidence
and is also nonbinding
([FDA September 2023 draft](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/demonstrating-substantial-evidence-effectiveness-one-adequate-and-well-controlled-clinical)).

The useful analogy is institutional, not legal: one pivotal positive should not authenticate
itself. INT-R9 requires independent owner receipts, public regression, a separately sealed
adjacent case, falsifiers, and human adjudication. It does not borrow FDA authority,
product-law standards, or a presumption that one result is sufficient.

### 3.8 Benchmark contamination and fresh cases

Recht et al. rebuilt CIFAR-10 and ImageNet test sets and observed substantial accuracy
drops, while cautioning that the new sets were somewhat harder and that adaptivity alone
did not explain the difference
([ICML/PMLR 2019](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/recht19a.html)).
Yang et al. show that literal overlap tests can miss contamination through paraphrase or
translation and argue for fresh one-time exams
([arXiv:2311.04850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04850)).

INT-R9 therefore requires a new case and semantic/provenance checks, not merely a grep for
the case identifier. It also refuses to treat one adjacent fresh case as a representative
sample. The adjacent result is a bounded transfer probe.

### 3.9 Human agreement and disagreement

Cohen's κ was introduced for nominal agreement between two raters
([DOI 10.1177/001316446002000104](https://doi.org/10.1177/001316446002000104)).
Krippendorff reviews reliability measures, their assumptions, and common misuse
([DOI 10.1111/j.1468-2958.2004.tb00738.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-2958.2004.tb00738.x)).
These measures can reveal an unstable guide or disagreement pattern. They cannot tell
whether all raters share the same mistaken premise. INT-R9 therefore publishes raw votes,
abstentions, and criterion-level dissent; coefficients are diagnostics, not promotion
tokens.

### 3.10 Publication bias and commitment machinery

Rosenthal's file-drawer formulation captures the distortion created when null results are
missing
([Psychological Bulletin 1979, DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.86.3.638](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.86.3.638)).
INT-R9's response is a signed, result-independent publication commitment and an immutable
attempt denominator. It cannot, by itself, make every career incentive benign; it can make
suppression visible and nonconforming.

For sealing, a secure hash digest detects change but does not by itself hide a predictable
answer
([NIST FIPS 180-4, DOI 10.6028/NIST.FIPS.180-4](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.FIPS.180-4)).
Commitment schemes distinguish binding from hiding; Damgård, Pedersen, and Pfitzmann give a
primary treatment of statistically hiding commitments
([BRICS 1996, DOI 10.7146/brics.v3i45.20047](https://doi.org/10.7146/brics.v3i45.20047)).
RFC 3161 provides a proof-of-existence timestamping protocol while expressly leaving broader
TSA security policy to operators
([RFC 3161, DOI 10.17487/RFC3161](https://doi.org/10.17487/RFC3161)).
The exact mechanism remains S0-GAP-02's responsibility; INT-R9 states the required
properties.

## 4. Result — FirstPromotionEvaluationProtocol

### 4.1 Epistemic classification of the result

| Kind | INT-R9 result |
| --- | --- |
| **Theorem reused, not proved here** | Any numeric false-promotion guarantee is inherited from the exact admitted confidence-ledger owner/profile, predictable schedule, scope, and maintained assumptions. INT-R9 adds no probability theorem. |
| **Impossibility result** | The current 15 public adjudications cannot be made into a sealed holdout retroactively; a role identifier cannot establish independent human adjudication; one event cannot identify population external validity; no protocol can promise that a positive exists; governed records cannot prove the absence of secret collusion. |
| **Governance protocol** | Prospective selection, sealing, immutable attempt order, result-independent publication, independent signed adjudication, deviation handling, no-substitution, correction, and bounded public wording. |
| **Design pattern** | Registered-report-style acceptance before outcomes; public regression plus separately held decisive cases; append-only attempt and amendment ledgers; adjacent unseen transfer; positive controls that do not count as real promotions. |
| **Empirical rule** | Fresh cases and hidden expectations reduce known contamination; reviewer calibration can expose ambiguity; neither creates truth by itself. |
| **Engineering convenience** | Six eligible primary–adjacent pairs, three selected attempt slots, three adjudicators, and two alternates. These numbers are finite and auditable, not uniquely correct. |

### 4.2 The claim the protocol is allowed to prove

A successful record may support only:

> Under protocol `P`, implementation revision `R`, environment `E`, selected case pair
> `C`, source/evidence cutoff `T`, evaluator `V`, confidence-ledger scope `L`, declared
> obligation basis `O`, and maintained assumptions `A`, the earliest eligible slot
> satisfied the named canonical owner predicates and INT-R9 procedural predicates; the
> public and adjacent tests behaved as recorded; the named human panel found no material
> unresolved dispute; and no prohibited selection or case-specific mechanism was found in
> the governed evidence.

It may not be shortened to “PolicyOS works,” “PolicyOS found a good policy,” “the system is
ready,” or “the result generalizes.”

### 4.3 Preconditions

Before any first-promotion case is drafted or selected for scoring, all of the following
must exist:

1. an exact repository, source-tree, build, dependency, model/prompt, configuration,
   registry, evaluator-executable, environment, and source-query freeze mechanism;
2. S0-GAP-02 or a consolidation-approved equivalent providing commitment, hiding,
   independently verifiable transaction time, access control, access logging, dual-control
   reveal, reviewer rotation/succession, challenge, and inter-reviewer adjudication;
3. three named adjudicators and two named alternates satisfying §4.9;
4. an INT-R1 `ObligationSetDeclaration`, even if its posture is weak or blocking;
5. a signed commitment to publish every attempted, refused, void, disputed, promoted,
   retired, or exhausted outcome;
6. a predeclared incentive rule prohibiting outcome-contingent compensation, performance
   credit, or case selection based on `useful_design_rate`;
7. a case-selection specification and eligible frame independent of implementation
   outputs.

Missing items 2, 3, or 5 automatically block sealing. A weak INT-R1 result does not destroy
the anti-selection protocol; it narrows the claim or creates a predeclared NO-GO.

### 4.4 Case construction and selection

The protocol governs a finite sequence without pretending that case authorship is a random
sample from all policies.

#### Step C1 — freeze the selection specification

Before case authors see implementation outputs, ratify a `CaseSelectionSpecification`
containing:

- purpose and external-validity boundary;
- eligible policy-matter frame and cutoff date;
- three predeclared strata that create meaningful diversity rather than a claimed
  representative sample;
- inclusion/exclusion predicates and materiality rules;
- primary–adjacent pairing rule;
- conflicts and independence requirements for case authors, eligibility reviewers, and
  custodian;
- a public-random selection algorithm, exact future randomness source/round, and tie rule;
- authoritative-for and may-not-use-for boundaries.

If no defensible external frame exists, the case pool may still be purposively authored,
but the public claim must say so. The protocol then proves absence of post-result
substitution within the declared pool, not absence of upstream topic-selection judgment.

#### Step C2 — create and commit a six-pair pool

An independent case unit creates six primary–adjacent pairs, two per stratum. For each pair
it produces separate input and expectation/evaluator packages. It cannot contribute to the
implementation, criteria, threshold choice, or outcome-contingent project reward. A
separate eligibility reviewer checks the sealed metadata against the selection
specification without seeing PolicyOS outputs.

Six pairs are an engineering convenience: enough to keep the final three-slot queue from
being selected wholly by author discretion, but small enough to author and custody
seriously. Consolidation may choose another finite pool size if the same property holds.

#### Step C3 — commit before selection

The custodian commits all six pairs, authorship/conflict records, eligibility decisions,
and the selection frame. Commitments must be both binding and hiding for the relevant
answer space. A plain unsalted digest is insufficient when answers are predictable.

#### Step C4 — choose three and order them without discretion

After commitments exist and before any primary input is revealed, the predeclared public
randomness round selects one pair per stratum and determines the three-slot order. The
`SelectionExecutionReceipt` records the committed frame, source/round, raw randomness,
algorithm, selected pairs, order, verifier, and transaction time. No person may reorder the
slots because one looks easier.

The remaining three pairs stay sealed until the protocol version is terminal. Their later
use or publication follows a predeclared rule; they cannot be inserted as replacement
slots.

### 4.5 What is preregistered

The `FirstPromotionPreRegistration` binds, at minimum:

- protocol, repository, source-tree, build/dependency, environment, model/prompt,
  configuration, evaluator, rule, and schema versions;
- eligible frame, exclusions, contamination census, case-pair commitments, selection
  receipt, three-slot order, stopping, and no-substitution rule;
- every existing canonical owner predicate and threshold reference, without redefining
  it;
- procedural predicates in §4.8;
- materiality, ambiguity, deviation, retry, exclusion, and amendment rules;
- public regression population and mutations;
- source-flip and obligation-removal targets and acceptable semantic relations;
- no-case-specific-code evidence requirements;
- panel, alternates, custodian, authors, contributors, conflicts, and independence
  assessments;
- result-independent publication, raw-vote, dissent, challenge, correction, and public
  wording rules;
- Custody Time Model roles and the temporal ordering proof;
- exact `authoritative_for` and `may_not_use_for` boundaries.

A git commit by the implementation team is not enough. The record's independently visible
transaction time must precede first input disclosure, output-bearing execution, evaluator
answer access, or human inspection.

### 4.6 Attempt order and stopping

The selected queue contains exactly three slots. “First” means the earliest slot that
reaches a valid positive terminal under its own frozen revision and the cumulative ledger.

1. Run slot 1 only after its implementation/environment freeze and public regression.
2. Stop permanently if slot 1 validly promotes.
3. If slot 1 is publicly refused or void, general implementation repair may occur before
   slot 2 input reveal; criteria, selected cases, order, stopping, publication, and
   cumulative risk scope remain fixed.
4. If slot 1 is materially disputed, halt. Slot 2 cannot become “first uncontested” while
   slot 1 remains unresolved.
5. Repeat for slots 2 and 3.
6. If all three terminate without promotion, publish
   `exhausted_without_promotion` and stop the protocol version.

An unregistered case that succeeds after a registered failure is development evidence only.
A retry may replace an attempt only when a predeclared deterministic infrastructure-failure
rule proves that no result-bearing output or answer was exposed. Otherwise the first
result-bearing run remains scored.

### 4.7 Public regression and implementation freeze

Before each sealed input reveal, the exact implementation revision must pass the
predeclared public battery:

- all 13 real cases;
- both synthetic adjudications;
- opaque-ID, delivery-order, semantically equivalent encoding, wrong-scope, source-flip,
  and obligation-removal public mutations;
- known-groundable seeds for false-abstention/search-recall monitoring;
- a deterministic contract-level positive control showing that the technical path is not
  permanently locked;
- one visible deliberately hand-coded fixture expected to fail the no-case-specific-code
  property.

The public battery is visible regression evidence, never decisive holdout evidence. Its
positive control does not count as a real policy promotion or enter the
`useful_design_rate` numerator.

The freeze includes source and generated source, build outputs, dependency lock and
resolved artifacts, container/environment image, model and prompt, templates, feature
flags, configuration, adapters, aliases, registries, bindings, evidence dictionaries,
source/query cutoff, caches, evaluator executable, and random-seed policy. After primary
input reveal, any change to these assets makes the slot void or disputed; it cannot salvage
the score.

### 4.8 Promotion predicates

A slot may promote only when **all applicable existing owner gates** and **all procedural
predicates** pass.

| Predicate | Observable property |
| --- | --- |
| `prospective_ordering` | Independent transaction evidence proves seal and queue selection preceded first inspection. |
| `next_committed_slot` | The evaluated case is the earliest unresolved slot; no earlier slot was omitted or replaced. |
| `public_regression_conformance` | The frozen revision passes every predeclared visible predicate before holdout reveal. |
| `canonical_owner_passage` | N9/waist/firewall/confidence and every applicable grounding, evidence, value, calibration, transport, delegation, and evaluation-safety owner permits the exact bounded claim. Unknown or scope-insufficient is not pass. |
| `sealed_primary_correctness` | The frozen revision produces the sealed-evaluator-correct primary behavior and, where positive, a valid canonical promotion receipt. |
| `adjacent_transfer` | The same frozen binary/configuration produces the sealed-evaluator-correct terminal behavior on the paired adjacent case. The correct behavior may be refusal or limitation. |
| `no_case_specific_mechanism` | Provenance, freeze equality, registry/adapter audit, opaque-ID mutation, semantic-fingerprint review, and adjacent behavior disclose no case-conditioned code, configuration, binding, prompt, alias, or fixture. |
| `source_dependency_sensitivity` | A predesignated material source flip cannot leave the same positive authority claim intact. |
| `obligation_monotonicity` | Removing, invalidating, or making unknown a required material obligation cannot leave the same positive promotion intact. |
| `independent_adjudication` | Named human panel satisfies identity, conflict, calibration, quorum, raw-vote, dissent, and succession rules. |
| `result_independent_publication` | Earlier attempts, raw votes, deviations, disputes, refusals, and denominator remain published. |
| `bounded_claim` | Public language names revision, environment, cases, evaluator, protocol, assumptions, unknowns, and external-validity boundary. |

A passing fixture is evidence for the property it samples; it never becomes the property
definition. Semantically equivalent implementations are allowed. Runtime enum names, one
graph topology, one scheduler, or one state machine are not ground truth.

### 4.9 Independent adjudicator: operational definition

The current role IDs do not satisfy this requirement. For INT-R9, an adjudicator must be an
identified, accountable natural person who signs the record and is independent of:

1. implementation and case-specific bindings;
2. case selection and case/answer authorship;
3. criteria, threshold, and materiality authorship;
4. answer-key custody before authorized reveal;
5. direct line management of evaluated implementers;
6. outcome-contingent compensation, funding, or project reward;
7. prior access to the scored output or answer outside the authorized sequence.

The panel has three members and two predeclared alternates. Collectively it should cover
method/evidence, domain/authority, and custody/public-claim competence. Promotion requires
at least two signed approvals, no dispute vote, no refusal grounded in a material criterion,
no unresolved material dissent, and a documented reason for every abstention. A missing
member may be replaced only by a predeclared alternate whose access/conflict record remains
clean; otherwise the attempt is disputed.

Before sealing, the panel calibrates on all 15 public adjudications. Publish raw votes,
abstentions, criterion-level disagreements, pairwise agreement, Cohen's κ where its
assumptions fit, Krippendorff's α where its assumptions fit, and guide amendments. No
universal numerical coefficient floor is imported. A materially ambiguous promotion
criterion must be resolved prospectively or the protocol cannot seal.

A synthetic reviewer, model, agent, or role-only identifier may assist public regression or
internal critique. It can never satisfy independent human adjudication for the first
promotion.

### 4.10 Sealed primary and expectation packages

The input package is revealed only after implementation freeze. It excludes the expected
terminal decision, case-specific expected claim identifiers where they reveal the answer,
gold card, votes, and answer-bearing evaluator details.

The separate expectation/evaluator package contains:

- expected predicate outcomes and admissible alternatives;
- criterion materiality decisions;
- source-flip and obligation-removal targets and expected relations;
- adjacency declaration;
- adjudication guide and evaluator version;
- known uncertainty and permissible abstention.

It is revealed only after candidate output, environment, source snapshot, and execution
records are frozen. S0-GAP-02 owns canonical serialization, hiding/binding commitment,
key/secret management, access policy, dual-control reveal, rotation, challenge, and
incident response. Any credible answer leakage before the relevant freeze makes the slot
void or disputed and leaves it in chronology and denominator.

### 4.11 Adjacent unseen case

Each primary case has one separately authored and separately committed adjacent case. The
pair shares a predeclared mechanism or policy-problem family but differs materially on at
least two dimensions such as jurisdiction, time, source family, affected population,
administrative form, implementation context, or evidence quality. It cannot be a paraphrase,
identifier replacement, or delivery-order mutation.

The exact same frozen implementation, model/prompt, configuration, adapter/registry set,
evaluator executable, and rule versions run the adjacent case. No adjacent-specific patch
is allowed. The expected outcome may be positive, limited, unknown, or refused. Requiring a
second positive would quietly optimize `useful_design_rate` and reward overclaiming.

A correct adjacent result supports one bounded transfer relation. It does not establish
representativeness or domain-wide external validity.

### 4.12 No-case-specific-code check

The check is a property and evidence bundle, not a grep rule. It requires:

- pre-reveal/post-run equality receipts for every frozen executable and configuration asset;
- literal case/source/claim ID scan;
- semantic fingerprint, source URL, alias, embedding, and binding review;
- registry and adapter delta proof;
- provenance for every relevant binding: author, first commit, purpose, source inputs,
  known case exposure, and edits;
- opaque identifier mutation;
- same-binary adjacent execution;
- signed maintainer declarations, treated as evidence but never sufficient alone;
- historical investigation of bindings authored by departed contributors.

A direct branch, hash/fingerprint branch, case-only adapter, hidden fixture, old hand-coded
binding, or post-reveal model/prompt/configuration change is an automatic NO-GO. The fact
that the contributor has left does not cleanse the binding.

### 4.13 Source-flip and obligation-removal falsifiers

The **source flip** preserves transport shape and unrelated evidence while revoking,
reversing, superseding, changing scope/effective time, or invalidating one predesignated
material dependency. The same positive authority claim may not survive unchanged. An
acceptable owner response may be refusal, unknown, limitation, suspension, narrower scope,
or revalidation.

The **obligation removal** removes, invalidates, or makes unknown one material obligation or
certificate declared by the INT-R1 interface. A positive promotion under the same authority
claim may not survive. INT-R9 does not infer that this sampled obligation exhausts the open
world.

Both targets and acceptable semantic relations are sealed before inspection. A fixture-ID
special case does not pass: opaque identities and independently authored variants accompany
the probes.

### 4.14 Enforcement surface for the `useful_design_rate` ban

The metric may be reported descriptively with an immutable denominator. It may not affect:

- eligible frame, case pool, selected cases, or order;
- criteria, threshold, materiality, or ambiguity interpretation;
- fixture inclusion/exclusion;
- run/seed selection, retry, or stopping;
- reviewer appointment, compensation, or evaluation;
- implementer/manager performance evaluation;
- public visibility or publication timing;
- promotion decision.

Observable evidence of forbidden optimization includes easier-case substitution, hidden
reruns, post-result threshold or materiality changes, failed/void attempt exclusion,
denominator edits, outcome-contingent rewards, stopping outside the fixed rule, and
case-specific repair after reveal. Every precommitted slot enters the denominator once
inspected; no post-result exclusions exist.

### 4.15 Preventing abstention from becoming dominant

Rule 5 and T6 create opposite risks: forcing positive credit is dishonest, but rewarding
only refusal produces paralysis. INT-R9 does not solve grounding economics, yet it can make
strategic refusal visible:

1. every refusal cites exact owner-backed reasons and evidence; generic safety language is
   nonconforming;
2. public known-groundable seeds measure false abstention and search recall;
3. a deterministic contract-level positive control detects a mechanically locked positive
   path without counting as a real promotion;
4. a failed primary emits an acquisition/repair record where an existing owner supports
   one;
5. no “domain ceiling” may be declared while search recall, index freshness, source access,
   or evidence acquisition is inadequate;
6. people are assessed on protocol integrity, evidence acquisition, and honest repair—not
   the promotion numerator;
7. refusal receives the same public visibility and completion credit as promotion.

These controls make refusal inspectable. They do not require a positive case to exist.

### 4.16 State machine

The full state machine, owners, clocks, expiry, escalation, terminal meanings, reopening,
and typed sketches are in
[state-machine-and-artifact-contracts.md](int-r9/state-machine-and-artifact-contracts.md).
The core path is:

```text
pre_registration_drafted
  -> sealed
  -> candidate_inspected
  -> adjudicated
  -> promoted | refused | disputed
```

Additional honest terminals are `void`, `retired_before_inspection`,
`terminal_no_attempt`, and `exhausted_without_promotion`. Workflow phase is a custody fact,
not a parallel runtime authority status. Later correction appends suspension, withdrawal,
correction, supersession, or challenge records through existing owners; it never erases the
historical first event.

### 4.17 Interface with INT-R1

INT-R9 consumes a versioned `ObligationSetDeclaration` with purpose/scope, obligations or
classes, bounded-completeness posture, maintained assumptions, known unknowns, criticality,
owners, and authority boundary.

- If INT-R1 supports the required bounded claim, evaluation may proceed within that scope.
- If INT-R1 is weaker, narrow the criteria and public claim.
- If a material obligation class is unknown or the declaration says the basis is
  insufficient, the slot is a NO-GO.
- The anti-selection record remains valid even when substantive promotion is blocked.

INT-R9 does not answer whether the obligation set is complete in an open world.

## 5. Counterexamples And Failure Modes

### 5.1 Adversarial reading by mechanism

| Mechanism | Rule-following attack | Required defeat | Residual limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Preregistration | Publish a vague plan before the run, then call result-favorable interpretations “clarifications.” | Materiality, ambiguity, deviation, and interpretation rules are sealed; material ambiguity creates dispute; clarification applies only prospectively. | No text eliminates every future ambiguity. |
| Case selection | Independent authors hand-pick six cases they expect to be easy. | Declared external frame, eligibility rules, conflict separation, committed pool, and public-random selection; disclose any remaining purposive judgment. | Without an objective frame, upstream topic-selection bias cannot be disproved. |
| Fixed order | Run later slots privately and report the best as the first. | Input release controlled by custodian; execution ordinals, source access, and transaction logs; later slot cannot start before prior terminal. | Fabricated logs or collusion remain fraud risks. |
| Sealing | Commit an unsalted hash of a tiny answer space or leak answers through issue titles/metadata. | Hiding commitment, high-entropy randomness, channel-wide access controls, dual reveal, incident attestations, side-channel review. | No finite control proves absence of covert disclosure. |
| Public regression | Tune to every public fixture until all are green. | Treat as regression only; require separately sealed primary and adjacent packages. | Public regressions still matter for known failures but supply no fresh evidence. |
| Independent review | Appoint friendly “external” reviewers employed by the same manager or paid for success. | Named humans, reporting/funding/outcome disclosures, no line-management or contingent reward, signed panel and alternates. | Undisclosed relationships remain possible. |
| Inter-rater calibration | Obtain high agreement because all raters share the same mistaken rubric. | Preserve dissent and substantive evidence; coefficients are diagnostics, not correctness proof. | A unanimously wrong panel remains possible. |
| No-case-specific code | Replace a literal case-ID branch with a source URL, hash, alias, or embedding fingerprint. | Semantic/provenance review, opaque IDs, registry audit, old-binding history, same-binary adjacent case. | Formal proof against every semantic shortcut is unavailable. |
| Adjacent case | Use a paraphrase of the primary so the same shortcut passes twice. | Predeclared adjacency differences, separate authorship/commitment, independently reviewed relation. | One pair remains weak external-validity evidence. |
| Source flip | Refuse only the known red-team source ID. | Hidden target, opaque identity, independently authored semantic mutations, evaluate owner relation. | One sampled dependency does not prove all dependency propagation. |
| Obligation removal | Fail only when a field is absent but accept an invalid or unknown certificate. | Vary semantic removal modes; inspect owner state rather than serialization. | One sampled obligation does not prove open-world completeness. |
| First run | Keep rerunning stochastic execution until a favorable output appears. | First result-bearing run scored; predeclared retry rule; immutable seed/run/source logs. | Physical systems can still falsify logs. |
| Failure exclusion | Call a failed fixture corrupt after seeing it fail. | P34 rule: retain the failure; only prospective version change after completed owner-backed isolation. | Genuine fixture defects may delay later work. |
| Publication | Publish success prominently and bury refusals in internal logs. | Signed outcome-neutral publication commitment and same public artifact surface for every terminal. | Informal attention and prestige asymmetry cannot be fully eliminated. |
| `useful_design_rate` | Keep rules fixed but reward managers for a rising numerator. | Disclose incentives; prohibit outcome-contingent rewards; audit case/order/stopping/denominator changes. | Organizational pressure may persist outside governed channels. |

### 5.2 Insider wants a positive result and follows every written rule

The strongest compliant adversary acts before the result: writes cases similar to known
bindings, chooses favorable strata, drafts vague criteria, appoints nominally external
friends, uses a weak “hash,” and rewards the team for a positive rate. A decorative protocol
would pass because every box was checked.

INT-R9 defeats the *observable* version of this attack by separating implementation,
criteria, case authorship, eligibility review, custody, and adjudication; committing a
larger pool before public-random selection; requiring hiding rather than mere hashing;
fixing materiality and ambiguity; auditing incentives; and retaining every attempt. The
protocol still cannot prove that all named people did not secretly collude. That residual
is an explicit limit, not an omitted caveat.

### 5.3 Insider wants to avoid blame and refuses everything

A constant-refusal implementation can appear perfectly honest if the protocol rewards only
safety. This realizes constitution tradeoff T6: the internal incentive gradient points away
from difficult grounding
(`policy-engine/docs/system-design-decisions/universal-policy-design-system-vision-and-organizing-rules.md:300-455`).

The countermeasures are outcome-neutral positive controls, known-groundable search-recall
seeds, owner-specific refusal reasons, acquisition/repair records, and a ban on domain-
ceiling claims under poor recall/freshness. None forces a real-policy positive. A system may
pass the controls and still honestly refuse every selected case.

### 5.4 Hand-coded binding from three slices ago

Suppose an author who has since left wrote a source-to-lever mapping for a case that later
enters the sealed pool. Nobody currently knows it is bespoke; the final run passes every
runtime gate. The no-case-specific-code property still fails. Provenance and historical
commit context, not current intent or employment, decide contamination. The slot is NO-GO,
remains in the attempt ledger, and cannot be replaced by the next convenient success.

### 5.5 An honest negative result

Suppose slot 1 reaches a canonical refusal because a material evidence owner is unknown,
the adjacent case also refuses correctly, and every custody condition passes. This is not
an evaluation failure. The protocol publishes the refusal, owner reason, acquisition path,
raw votes, and denominator, then permits general repair before slot 2 reveal. It does not
change criteria or rerun slot 1 as confirmatory.

Suppose all three slots refuse. The terminal is `exhausted_without_promotion`, not
“inconclusive benchmark.” The result means that this finite program, under its versions and
assumptions, produced no positive promotion. It does not mean that positive promotion is
impossible.

## 6. Benchmark Or Fixture Proposal

The detailed specifications and bounded meanings are in
[fixture-specifications.md](int-r9/fixture-specifications.md). This section states the
minimum evaluation architecture.

### 6.1 Four evidence layers

1. **Visible public regression.** All 13 real cases, 2 synthetic adjudications, and public
   mutations. Purpose: prevent known regressions and calibrate humans. Never called a
   holdout.
2. **Sealed primary.** A newly authored case selected prospectively from the committed
   pool. Purpose: decisive one-time evidence for the named revision.
3. **Sealed adjacent.** A separately authored paired case, run with the same freeze.
   Purpose: one bounded transfer check and anti-bespoke evidence.
4. **Sealed falsifiers.** Source flip, obligation removal, wrong-scope look-alike, and
   opaque-identity variants. Purpose: test semantic properties rather than memorize one
   answer.

### 6.2 Sealed-holdout construction

A valid holdout requires:

- input and expectation/evaluator packages separated;
- canonical serialization;
- binding and hiding commitment with high-entropy randomness or another approved hiding
  construction;
- independent custodian signature and independently verifiable transaction time;
- exact purpose and access principals;
- immutable access logs across file storage, issue/review systems, model context, chat,
  screenshots, metadata, and other governed disclosure channels;
- dual-control answer reveal;
- implementation/environment/source/evaluator-executable freeze before input reveal;
- candidate-output freeze before expectation reveal;
- reveal-verification receipt;
- leakage incident and challenge procedure;
- raw expectation versions retained after reveal.

This is **constructively possible in principle but unavailable in the current repository**.
S0-GAP-02 must choose and govern the cryptographic and access architecture. The current
git-visible answers cannot be re-sealed.

### 6.3 Source-flip falsifier

Property: material source dependency remains live.

Construction: preserve transport shape, unrelated facts, and cryptographic validity where
relevant; change one predeclared material dependency by revocation, reversal, supersession,
scope change, effective-time change, or credibility invalidation.

Pass relation: the same positive authority claim does not survive unchanged. The canonical
owner may refuse, downgrade, narrow, suspend, or require revalidation.

Bounded meaning: one sampled dependency relation, not universal source sensitivity.

### 6.4 Obligation-removal falsifier

Property: promotion is monotone with respect to required material obligations.

Construction: preserve unrelated evidence; remove, invalidate, or make unknown one
predeclared required obligation/certificate or its owner theorem.

Pass relation: no positive promotion under the same authority claim.

Bounded meaning: one sampled declared obligation; no open-world completeness claim.

### 6.5 No-case-specific-code evidence

The audit package includes freeze equality, dependency/configuration equality, identifier
and semantic-fingerprint scan, binding provenance, adapter/registry delta, opaque-ID rerun,
same-binary adjacent execution, and historical contributor review. Absence of a literal ID,
a passing unit test, or a contributor declaration alone cannot pass.

### 6.6 Adjacent-unseen design

The pairing declaration is committed before reveal and names what is shared, what differs,
and why the pair is not a paraphrase. At least two material context dimensions differ.
Correct terminal behavior—not a second positive—is required. A different binary or
configuration is automatic failure.

### 6.7 Required edge cases

| Edge case | Predeclared disposition |
| --- | --- |
| Registered case fails; unregistered case succeeds | Registered slot remains refused. Unregistered success is exploratory and cannot become first. |
| Adjudicator unavailable mid-adjudication | Use only a clean predeclared alternate; otherwise dispute. |
| Criterion becomes ambiguous after sealing | Material ambiguity creates dispute; no favorable reinterpretation; clarification is future-version only. |
| Sealed holdout leaks | Void or dispute; keep slot in chronology, denominator, and risk scope; no replacement. |
| Promotion later found unjustified | Append challenge and canonical suspension/correction/withdrawal/supersession; preserve original history. |
| Two candidates appear to qualify simultaneously | Starting the later slot before the earlier terminal is nonconforming; firstness follows committed order and canonical transaction time. |
| Preregistration mis-specified before any result is seen | Retire old version only with affirmative no-inspection/no-answer-access proof; publish diff and reseal. |
| Old hand-coded binding discovered | Automatic NO-GO regardless of author departure or runtime passage. |
| Hidden rerun produces favorable output | First result-bearing run remains scored; later run cannot replace it. |
| Failed fixture excluded after outcome | Retain failure unless a completed, owner-backed isolation proof already governed the exclusion; redesign applies prospectively only. |
| Earlier slot disputed; later case looks positive | Halt; later case cannot become first until earlier dispute resolves. |

### 6.8 What a passing battery means

A passing battery means only that the named revision, environment, selected cases,
mutations, evaluator, protocol, owners, and assumptions satisfied the tested predicates.
It does not prove the oracle is infallible, the cases are representative, all obligations
were known, every source dependency is live, every bespoke mechanism was detected, or a
future revision will pass.

## 9. Promotion And Kill Rules

### 9.1 Formal promotion rule

For selected slot `s_i`, procedural promotion is admissible only if:

```text
Promote(s_i) iff
  IsEarliestUnresolvedCommittedSlot(s_i)
  AND ProspectiveOrderingProved(s_i)
  AND SelectionAndStoppingConform(s_i)
  AND PublicRegressionPasses(frozen_revision_i)
  AND EveryApplicableCanonicalOwnerPermitsExactClaim(s_i)
  AND ConfidenceLedgerReceiptValidAndInScope(s_i)
  AND CandidateFirewallValid(s_i)
  AND SealedPrimaryEvaluatorCorrect(s_i)
  AND SameFreezeAdjacentEvaluatorCorrect(s_i)
  AND NoCaseSpecificMechanismFound(s_i)
  AND SourceFlipRelationPasses(s_i)
  AND ObligationRemovalRelationPasses(s_i)
  AND NamedIndependentPanelAdmits(s_i)
  AND NoMaterialUnresolvedDissent(s_i)
  AND NoPredeclaredNoGoReasonApplies(s_i)
  AND ResultIndependentPublicationComplete(s_i)
  AND PublicClaimWithinBoundedExternalValidity(s_i)
```

A false value or unknown in any conjunct blocks promotion. INT-R9 cannot override a lower
canonical owner boundary.

### 9.2 Predeclared NO-GO reasons

A slot cannot promote if any of the following occurs:

1. preregistration, pool commitment, selection, or criterion freeze is later than first
   outcome-relevant inspection;
2. the case is not the next committed slot;
3. a current public corpus case is used as the decisive sealed holdout;
4. ua-msme is used as decisive primary or adjacent evidence;
5. role-only/synthetic identities are presented as independent adjudicators;
6. named-human quorum is missing or material dissent remains;
7. answer custody is missing, unverifiable, weakly hiding, or leaked;
8. source, code, generated source, build, dependency, model, prompt, configuration, adapter,
   binding, registry, evaluator logic, query, or cutoff changes after reveal;
9. case-specific or old hand-coded binding is found;
10. a hidden rerun, seed selection, slot omission, result-based substitution, or
    outcome-conditioned reviewer replacement occurs;
11. threshold, criterion, fixture, materiality, ambiguity, exclusion, or stopping changes
    after result-bearing access;
12. public regression fails;
13. source flip leaves the same positive authority claim intact;
14. obligation removal leaves the same positive promotion intact;
15. adjacent case uses a different freeze or produces sealed-evaluator-incorrect behavior;
16. a canonical owner is fail, unknown, scope-insufficient, stale, invalid, or bypassed;
17. confidence-ledger scope, head, profile, schedule, execution ordinal, allocation, receipt,
    or maintained assumptions are invalid;
18. INT-R1 says the material obligation basis is insufficient for the proposed claim;
19. result-independent publication is breached;
20. `useful_design_rate` affects any protected choice;
21. an attempted exclusion lacks a completed, prospective, owner-backed isolation proof;
22. public wording exceeds the named revision, environment, cases, evaluator, assumptions,
    and authority boundary.

### 9.3 What happens when the first attempt fails

A failed, void, or refused slot remains the first attempt. It is published before the next
input reveal. General implementation repair may occur for the next slot, but the case
commitments, selected order, criteria, thresholds, materiality, stopping, publication, and
cumulative confidence scope remain fixed. The failed case cannot be rerun as a new
confirmatory first-promotion attempt.

A material dispute halts the sequence. Proceeding to a later slot while the earlier one is
unresolved would make “first” selectable.

After all three slots terminate without promotion, the protocol version ends as
`exhausted_without_promotion`. A future v2 may be prospectively ratified with a new pool and
fresh cases, but it must retain v1, explain every change, preserve cumulative accounting
where the canonical ledger requires it, and never present v2 as if v1 did not occur.

### 9.4 Amendment rule

- **Before any inspection:** amendment is allowed only when the custodian affirmatively
  proves no primary/adjacent input, answer, output, or outcome-relevant information was
  accessed. Preserve and publish the retired version and diff; create new commitments and
  transaction times.
- **After inspection:** no amendment rescoring is allowed. Deviations remain evaluated under
  the sealed version. A changed protocol applies only to a future version and fresh cases.
- **After a positive:** changes are correction, suspension, withdrawal, supersession, or
  challenge actions through existing owners, not retroactive amendment of the scored
  protocol.

### 9.5 Protocol kill rules

Kill or prospectively redesign the protocol version if:

- the selected frame or randomness execution cannot be reproduced;
- S0-GAP-02 cannot provide meaningful hiding, access separation, or challenge custody;
- no named human panel satisfies independence;
- a promotion-critical criterion remains materially ambiguous after calibration;
- the public battery or sealed packages have been used for tuning;
- the no-case-specific-code property is reduced to a literal-ID scan;
- adjacent cases are paraphrases or require a different implementation;
- the procedure creates a parallel status, risk ledger, independence framework, or owner;
- a fixture is treated as the specification rather than a semantic sample;
- failed runs can be excluded after the result;
- success is required, rewarded, or implied;
- abstention can be generic and costless without owner-backed evidence;
- public wording would exceed S0-K16's bounded passage;
- the protocol conflicts with a ratified kernel statement and no approved reopening has
  occurred.

A killed fixture or version remains in completed run history. Redesign never erases its
failure.

### 9.6 Making no-promotion career-survivable

The governance body must sign, before sealing, that completion is assessed on adherence,
evidence quality, challenge response, and honest repair—not outcome sign. It must prohibit
promotion-contingent bonuses, milestone credit, reviewer payment, or manager evaluation.
Every terminal receives the same durable public artifact class, release channel, review
agenda, and archival priority. Queue exhaustion fulfills the research/evaluation mandate.

This cannot eliminate informal prestige differences. It makes formal retaliation or
suppression inconsistent with the preregistered record and visible to the later audit.

### 9.7 Current readiness decision

At `d152565dcc11cea457dacd61fadc6e15dc3ecc86`, do **not** inspect a first-promotion
candidate under a claim that INT-R9 is executable. The following remain absent or unmet:

- fresh committed primary/adjacent pool;
- approved selection-frame and public-random execution machinery;
- S0-GAP-02 delivered custody/evaluator architecture;
- named independent humans and signed conflicts;
- INT-R1 obligation declaration;
- implementation and environment freeze receipts for a real attempt;
- result-independent public commitment signed by the accountable governance body.

The research protocol is delivered; the first governed promotion remains unattempted.

## 10. Open Questions For Consolidation

### 10.1 INT-R1 seam

Minimum consumed artifact: versioned `ObligationSetDeclaration` with purpose, scope,
obligations/classes, bounded-completeness posture, maintained assumptions, known unknowns,
criticality/owner references, and `may_not_use_for`.

Open consolidation questions:

- Which obligation weakness blocks any positive claim versus merely narrows scope?
- How is a newly discovered material obligation represented after sealing without
  retroactively pretending the original set was complete?
- Does a void/refused attempt consume the same confidence-ledger position when the failure
  is procedural rather than substantive?

INT-R9 deliberately does not solve open-world completeness.

### 10.2 S0-GAP-02 overlap

**Reuse from S0-GAP-02:** oracle/evaluator separation, canonical package serialization,
hiding/binding commitment, independent time evidence, least-privilege access, access logs,
dual reveal, evaluator versioning, reviewer rotation/succession, challenge,
inter-reviewer adjudication, and raw-label/abstention/dispute/correction retention.

**Distinct INT-R9 delta:** finite precommitted first-event sequence, public-random selection
from a committed pool, no substitution, firstness ordering, no-case-specific-code evidence,
same-freeze adjacent case, result-independent refusal/exhaustion publication,
`useful_design_rate` enforcement surface, and later correction of the historically first
public claim.

**Defer to S0-GAP-02:** cryptographic mechanism, key/secret management, generic evaluator
platform, generic reviewer rotation policy, and access-control implementation.

Consolidation must reject any INT-R9 implementation that builds a second independence
framework.

### 10.3 Live INT-R5 and INT-R8 conflicts

- **INT-R5:** institutional competence, delegation, and named-person eligibility may affect
  whether a case author, evidence producer, custodian, or adjudicator can serve. INT-R9
  consumes those facts and cannot certify competence itself.
- **INT-R8:** public projection, disclosure, compression, challenge, and currentness semantics
  determine how positive, refused, void, disputed, exhausted, corrected, or withdrawn
  records appear. INT-R9 requires equal auditability and bounded wording but must not create
  a competing public-record model.

### 10.4 Duplicate-abstraction risks

Consolidation should search for and eliminate:

- a second `PromotionDecision` or promotion status alongside N9/waist owners;
- a second risk/alpha ledger alongside the confidence ledger;
- a second oracle/evaluator independence service alongside S0-GAP-02;
- a second time vocabulary instead of CTM roles;
- a second public status/currentness lattice instead of Atlas/existing owners;
- duplicate deviation, challenge, or correction records that disagree about current truth;
- a universal event envelope introduced only to transport INT-R9 artifacts.

The recommended consolidation owner is the Stage-0 custody-kernel/consolidation authority
working with the canonical GY, confidence-ledger, CTM, and Atlas owners—not a new INT-R9
runtime owner.

### 10.5 Unresolved design choices

1. **Eligible frame.** What external, auditable frame can generate cases without project-
   chosen tractability? If none exists, how prominently must purposive selection be stated?
2. **Finite numbers.** Are six pairs, three slots, three adjudicators, and two alternates the
   right operational costs? Their finiteness is load-bearing; the exact counts are not.
3. **Public randomness.** Which source, round-selection rule, outage behavior, and
   manipulation analysis does S0-GAP-02 approve?
4. **Semantic contamination.** How can the project assess prior model/pretraining exposure,
   paraphrase, source fingerprints, and old contributor knowledge without making an
   impossible “no exposure anywhere” claim?
5. **Adjacency.** Which owner defines mechanism-family adjacency and material difference
   without writing the pair around the expected outcome?
6. **Materiality.** Which existing owners decide that a source or obligation is material,
   and how are disagreements resolved before sealing?
7. **Human calibration.** With only 15 public manifests, what calibration design is
   sufficient to expose ambiguity without pretending to estimate stable reviewer
   performance?
8. **Void risk accounting.** The confidence ledger must decide whether and how a procedurally
   void attempt consumes allocation; INT-R9 forbids refunds but does not redefine theorem
   semantics.
9. **Nondeterminism.** Which failures are truly non-result-bearing infrastructure failures,
   and what evidence permits a retry without run selection?
10. **Unselected sealed pairs.** When and how are unused packages disclosed, retired, or
    preserved for future evaluation without creating adaptive leakage?
11. **Organizational incentives.** What evidence demonstrates that outcome-contingent rewards
    are absent across contractors, funders, managers, and reviewers?
12. **Post-promotion currentness.** Which canonical owner suspends or withdraws a first
    promotion during a material challenge, and how does Atlas show historical versus current
    truth?

### 10.6 Ratified-kernel reopening candidates

This research found no evidence requiring reopening S0-K13, S0-K15, or S0-K16. A future
reopening candidate would require a concrete falsifier—for example, proof that preserving
all failed runs or an adjacent unseen case makes the bounded property untestable while a
weaker mechanism provides equal anti-selection protection. Convenience, cost, or a desire
to promote ua-msme is not such a falsifier.

### 10.7 Consolidation verdict

The consolidation pass should accept the following kernel:

1. ua-msme and the other current cases are public regression, not decisive holdout;
2. firstness is the earliest qualifying slot in a prospectively committed finite sequence;
3. selected cases and order derive from a committed frame and non-discretionary execution;
4. primary, adjacent, expectation, and falsifier packages remain independently sealed;
5. named human adjudication is mandatory; role-only identities do not qualify;
6. existing canonical owner gates and confidence accounting remain authoritative;
7. source flip, obligation removal, no-case-specific-code, and adjacent transfer are
   semantic properties with bounded fixture evidence;
8. every negative and disputed result is public and immutable;
9. no positive outcome is assumed;
10. passage supports only the named bounded claim.

If consolidation cannot secure the prerequisites, the honest result is
`blocked_pending_operational_independence`, not a relaxed first-promotion ritual.
