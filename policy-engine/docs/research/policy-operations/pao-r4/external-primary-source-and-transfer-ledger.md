# External primary-source and transfer ledger

## 1. Use rule

This ledger imports boundary principles, not compliance conclusions. A cited regime binds only its
own scope, institutions, definitions, exceptions, and remedies. PAO-R4 deliberately chooses a
broader engineering trigger—**material contribution to a protected individual action**—where that
is necessary to prevent a technical bypass. Nothing here declares any PolicyOS artifact, export, or
future implementation lawful, sufficient, or compliant in any jurisdiction.

## 2. European Union

| Stable identifier | Primary proposition used | Transfer to PAO-R4 | What does not transfer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regulation (EU) 2016/679, **CELEX 32016R0679**, Articles 4(4), 13(2)(f), 14(2)(g), 15(1)(h), 22; Recital 71 | Profiling concerns automated processing used to evaluate personal aspects; Article 22 addresses certain solely automated decisions producing legal or similarly significant effects and supplies safeguards/exceptions. Information duties cover the existence of covered automated decision-making and meaningful information about logic/significance/consequences. | Separate population/model output from a protected individual act; require purpose visibility, safeguards, explanation evidence, and human intervention that is real rather than ceremonial. | PAO-R4 does not adopt GDPR territorial/material scope, Article 22's exact threshold, exceptions, lawful bases, controller/processor roles, or any compliance conclusion. Its material-contribution trigger is intentionally broader than “solely automated.” |
| CJEU, **Case C-634/21, SCHUFA Holding (Scoring), ECLI:EU:C:2023:957** | A score produced by one actor can fall within automated individual decision-making where a third party draws strongly on that score in establishing, implementing, or terminating a contractual relationship. | Formal separation between producer and final decider does not eliminate the producer-side boundary when downstream reliance is material. Returning evidence must reveal strong/material reliance, not only formal finality. | The judgment's interpretation is not converted into a universal definition for every policy domain, and no inference of GDPR compliance follows. |
| Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union, **2012/C 326/02**, Article 41(2)(a)-(c) | In its scope, good administration includes being heard, access to the file subject to legitimate confidentiality, and an obligation to give reasons. | A population explanation is not an individual reason. Case facts, actual grounds, hearing/review, and reason-giving remain external case-system duties; PolicyOS can require bounded evidence that they were not silently replaced by its artifact. | The Charter's institutional and personal scope, direct effect, remedies, and legal sufficiency do not transfer. |
| Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, **CELEX 32024R1689**, Article 86 | In the Regulation's stated conditions, affected persons may obtain clear and meaningful explanations of the role of a high-risk AI system in a decision and the main elements of the decision. | Evidence should distinguish artifact role from the final rule and case facts; role visibility is a returning-evidence obligation. | PAO-R4 does not classify any PolicyOS system under the AI Act, determine high-risk status, or claim Article 86 compliance. |

### EU transfer conclusion

The transferable result is that **upstream scoring or profiling cannot escape scrutiny merely because
another actor formally decides**, and individual safeguards concern the actual role and grounds of
the system. PAO-R4 is not weaker: it triggers on material contribution, includes human-mediated
recommendations, and refuses classes whose contribution cannot be observed.

## 3. Canada—federal public administration

| Stable identifier | Primary proposition used | Transfer to PAO-R4 | What does not transfer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat, **Directive on Automated Decision-Making**, policy instrument page identifier `id=32592` | The Directive applies impact-scaled requirements to federal administrative decisions supported by automated decision systems, including impact assessment, notice, explanation, testing, monitoring, human intervention, recourse, reporting, and release of relevant information. | Treat decision support, not only fully automated final acts, as boundary-relevant; scale evidence and safeguards to impact; make explanation, monitoring, intervention, and recourse observable. | The Directive's departmental scope, impact levels, exemptions, prescribed measures, and compliance assessment do not transfer. PAO-R4 creates no Algorithmic Impact Assessment score. |
| Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat, **Algorithmic Impact Assessment** associated with the Directive | A structured assessment asks about system, decision, data, impact, consultation, and mitigation characteristics before deployment and yields an impact level that drives Directive requirements. | Purpose and protected-action classification must occur before export/use, not after an incident. | The questionnaire, scoring weights, impact-level formula, and federal approval process are not adopted as PolicyOS contracts. |

### Canada transfer conclusion

The decisive transfer is **pre-use classification plus evidence-bearing operational safeguards**.
PAO-R4 goes further on observability: a voluntary or unreconciled downstream report cannot support a
firewall claim, regardless of whether an assessment document exists.

## 4. United States

| Stable identifier | Primary proposition used | Transfer to PAO-R4 | What does not transfer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Administrative Procedure Act, **5 U.S.C. § 555(e)** | Except in affirming a prior denial or where denial is self-explanatory, prompt notice of denial of a written application/petition/request must be accompanied by a brief statement of grounds. | A population statistic cannot be silently substituted for the actual grounds of an individual denial. Returning evidence should bind the artifact's role to the case reason rather than emit a generic model explanation. | The provision's agency/proceeding scope, exceptions, remedies, and sufficiency standard do not transfer; PAO-R4 does not draft reasons. |
| **Los Angeles Department of Water & Power v. Manhart, 435 U.S. 702 (1978)** | In the Title VII context, even a true generalization about a class does not justify treating an individual employee as though the generalization necessarily describes that person. | Statistical generalization over a protected or other class is not an individual fact. The firewall denies individual eligibility, amount, sanction, credibility, and risk use of population claims absent a separately justified individual procedure. | The case's Title VII holding and protected-class doctrine are not generalized into a universal legal rule or compliance conclusion. |
| Office of Management and Budget, **M-24-10**, “Advancing Governance, Innovation, and Risk Management for Agency Use of Artificial Intelligence” | Federal agencies are directed to use minimum risk-management practices for rights-impacting and safety-impacting AI, including testing, impact assessment, monitoring, notice, explanation, human oversight, and remedies/appeals in stated contexts. | Public-sector algorithmic accountability requires operational evidence around protected effects, not a model card alone. | The memorandum's agency scope, definitions, deadlines, waivers, and binding status do not become PolicyOS rules. |

### United States transfer conclusion

The transferable line is the separation between **general evidence and actual individual grounds**,
plus operational safeguards for rights-impacting uses. PAO-R4 does not decide the legal validity of
statistical evidence in any particular U.S. programme.

## 7. Final legal-transfer limitation

Citing these sources establishes that administrative-law and algorithmic-accountability regimes
have long treated the policy/model-to-person transition as legally significant and safeguard-heavy.
It does **not** establish that PAO-R4 is legally sufficient, that a future implementation complies,
that any export is permitted, or that PolicyOS should own the individual procedure. Jurisdiction-
specific counsel, competent administrative owners, and the external case system remain separate
prerequisites.
