# Oracle custody, authority axioms, access, challenge, and adjudication

## 1. The oracle is itself a custody subject

A sealed expectation is not timeless truth. It is a versioned research artifact whose creation, access, interpretation, correction, and use must be attributable. The benchmark is therefore invalid if it applies stronger custody expectations to the implementation than to its own oracle.

The governing lens distinguishes facts, capability, authority, institutional acts, implementation state, and legal sufficiency. A benchmark may stipulate a synthetic scenario and test observable behavior; it may not convert that scenario into proof that a real authority or institution acted. (`policy-engine/docs/system-design-decisions/stage0-custody-kernel-ratification.md:46-88@109ba3f44e09e0d34cf49ae19aa25ba4048ee3ee`, authority-band lens; `policy-engine/docs/system-design-decisions/stage0-custody-kernel-ratification.md:164-176@109ba3f44e09e0d34cf49ae19aa25ba4048ee3ee`, application note and finding `S0-K15`.)

## 2. Authority-scenario axioms

### 2.1 Axiom record

Each authority premise is a versioned, challengeable record:

```yaml
AuthorityScenarioAxiom:
  axiom_id: stable_identifier
  version: semver_like_string
  proposition: declarative_expression
  scope:
    synthetic_jurisdiction_profile: stable_identifier
    subject_kinds: [stable_identifier]
    operation_kinds: [stable_identifier]
    temporal_scope: interval_or_rule
  standing: stipulated | contested | unknown
  provenance:
    source_refs: [stable_identifier]
    derivation_digest: digest
    authoring_record_digest: digest
  permitted_inferences: [stable_identifier]
  forbidden_inferences: [stable_identifier]
  dissent_position_refs: [opaque_handle]
  abstention_refs: [opaque_handle]
  supersedes_axiom_digest: digest | null
  may_not_use_for: [string]
```

### 2.2 Required axiom families

The benchmark profile should be able to express, without prescribing production internals:

- **scope axioms:** tenant, cell, matter, purpose, subject, operation, and time boundaries;
- **authority-source axioms:** what assertion exists in the synthetic scenario and its stipulated effective interval;
- **revocation/narrowing axioms:** how a stipulated authority-only change differs from a payload change;
- **dependency axioms:** which public semantic relationships are declared, not how product code traverses them;
- **observation axioms:** which channels are visible to evaluators and when;
- **historicity axioms:** distinctions among effective time, observation time, transaction time, and run time;
- **external-act axioms:** which attempted operations count as observable protected acts;
- **ambiguity axioms:** finite branches caused by contested or missing facts;
- **non-inference axioms:** explicit statements that signatures, possession, syntax validity, or historical publication do not alone establish current authority.

### 2.3 Axiom and specification-assurance discipline

1. Axioms are synthetic premises for a test; they do not claim legal sufficiency.
2. Every permitted inference is paired with forbidden inferences so an evaluator cannot silently broaden the premise.
3. Contested propositions retain signed positions. They are not normalized to `true` merely to make a fixture executable.
4. An `unknown` premise either creates a bounded alternative branch or makes the fixture non-claimable.
5. A change to an axiom creates a new digest and supersession edge. It never rewrites the premise used by a prior run.
6. No axiom may be generated by observing current implementation behavior.
7. Reviewers may challenge the benchmark premise even when the implementation matches the sealed expectation.
8. The scenario author may not unilaterally derive and seal `O_v`. The `B` to `O_v` step uses an independent expectation derivation or documented dual control with separate signatures and conflict records.
9. Blinded specification-premise proficiency anchors and drift checks are required for the reviewer population; unanimity without passed scope-relevant proficiency does not establish the premise.
10. A shared-premise seed detected by `A-14` or `A-16` yields `SPECIFICATION_ASSURANCE_NOT_ESTABLISHED` and withholds the stronger claim.

```yaml
SpecificationAssuranceRecord:
  record_id: opaque_identifier
  public_specification_digest: digest
  expectation_bundle_digest: digest
  scope_profile_digest: digest
  scenario_author_record_digest: digest
  expectation_derivation_record_digests: [digest]
  dual_control_or_independent_derivation: boolean
  reviewer_proficiency_record_digests: [digest]
  drift_check_record_digests: [digest]
  dissent_and_abstention_digests: [digest]
  open_blocking_challenge_digests: [digest]
  predicate_provenance_class: institutionally_accepted | not_established
  disposition: assurance_established_for_named_scope | assurance_not_established
  signatures: [detached_signature]
```

The record does not machine-prove normative truth. `institutionally_accepted` remains an explicit `P37` provenance class and keeps the claim bounded to the named scope. `assurance_not_established` is a valid `INT-K08` negative completion, not a new outcome-vocabulary element.

## 3. Abstract roles and separation of duties

These are required role classes and incompatibilities, not appointments.

| Abstract role | Minimum function | Must not also perform for the same evaluation window |
|---|---|---|
| Scenario author | Draft public axioms and input-only fixtures. | Product implementation author; expectation author/sealer unless an independently validated dual-control derivation is used; sole adjudicator of a challenge to their own axiom. |
| Expectation author | Derive explicit alternatives, exclusions, predicates, and compiler proofs from public axioms. | Product implementation author; scenario author acting alone; run submitter; sole key custodian; sole adjudicator of their own bundle. |
| Specification-assurance reviewer | Review `B` to `O_v` derivation, proficiency results, dissent, and challenges. | Sole author of the reviewed axiom/expectation; product author; sole adjudicator. |
| Evaluator-R author/reviewer | Implement and review declarative reducer `R_v`. | Evaluator-P primary author; `M_v`/`J_v` primary author for relations they decide; product semantic author; plaintext expectation custodian before freeze. |
| Evaluator-P author/reviewer | Implement and review predicate/metamorphic evaluator `P_v`. | Evaluator-R primary author; `M_v` primary author or `J_v` primary validator for relations they decide; product semantic author; plaintext expectation custodian before freeze. |
| Mutation-generator author | Implement `M_v` from the public relation catalogue. | Primary `J_v` validator; primary R/P author for the same relation family; product semantic author. |
| Mutation relation validator | Independently validate mutation certificates and public relation application. | `M_v` primary author; R/P primary author deciding the same relation; product semantic author. |
| Commitment operator | Publish digests, Merkle roots, and signatures. | Unilateral plaintext editor; unilateral correction approver. |
| Key/access custodian | Control encrypted expectation access and key lifecycle. | Product submitter; sole evaluator; sole challenge adjudicator; unilateral log editor. |
| Run operator | Execute frozen artifacts and collect immutable traces. | Unilateral expectation editor; product revision mutator after freeze. |
| Reviewer proficiency administrator | Prepare blinded anchors and retain raw qualification/drift results. | Sole member of the reviewed population; expectation author for the same seeds. |
| Challenger | Submit evidence-backed objections. | No blanket incompatibility; conflicts must be declared and visible. |
| Adjudication function | Classify a challenge and record disposition. | Sole author of the challenged axiom, expectation, generator, relation, or evaluator behavior; person with an undeclared material conflict. |
| Log witness/auditor | Verify append-only history, access events, independent audit heads, and consistency proofs. | Sole log administrator or sole signing-key holder. |

```yaml
RoleAssignmentWindow:
  evaluation_window_ref: opaque_identifier
  role_assignments:
    - actor_ref: opaque_identifier
      role: stable_identifier
      artifact_or_relation_scope: [stable_identifier]
      conflict_declaration_digest: digest
  dual_control_records: [digest]
  validator_version: stable_identifier
  validation_findings: [stable_identifier]
  accepted: boolean
  signed_digest: digest
```

The role-assignment validator rejects every forbidden combination before artifact freeze. A waiver after observing product or evaluator results is forbidden. This is evidence for audit R9 and `A-20`; it does not appoint any person or institution.

NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 control `AC-5` supplies a general separation-of-duties pattern; GAO-24-106786 supplies public-audit independence and quality-management principles. The transfer is role incompatibility and evidence, not adoption of either regime as an accreditation decision.

## 4. Commitment and run sequence

The sequence is stateful and append-only:

1. **Public-spec and finite-domain freeze.** Publish `B`, finite trace-domain profile, predicate DSL, axiom profile, trace-observation profile, corpus manifest, and the frozen P37 predicate-provenance register.
2. **Role-window validation.** Validate the `RoleAssignmentWindow`, including independent/dual-control `B` to `O_v` derivation and M/J/R/P incompatibilities.
3. **Evaluator/generator freeze.** Independently build `R_v`, `P_v`, `M_v`, and `J_v`; publish source/artifact/SBOM/provenance digests.
4. **Discriminator and answer-neutrality qualification.** Commit discriminator witnesses and run liveness/removal/neutralization plus poisoned-helper probes for every semantic family.
5. **Reviewer/evaluator proficiency.** Run blinded product-side and specification-side seeds; preserve raw misses and drift evidence.
6. **Expectation and specification-assurance freeze.** Compile `O_v` under the decidable DSL, publish proof digests, commit the sealed bundle, and bind `S_v`. Plaintext remains restricted.
7. **Implementation freeze.** Record implementation revision, artifact digest, SBOM, environment, configuration, and submission timestamp.
8. **Post-freeze population derivation.** Generate hidden adjacent fixtures under committed `M_v` and seed rules; `J_v` independently validates certificates before product scoring.
9. **Execution.** Run `I`; capture immutable raw trace. Run `C` separately as diagnostic. `C` cannot satisfy verification.
10. **Independent evaluation.** Run `R_v` and `P_v` in isolated environments. Record reads, comparisons, findings, abstentions, and failures.
11. **Access reconciliation.** Reconcile oracle, storage, network, and key-service audit heads. Mismatch gives `RUN_INVALID`; unavailable decisive evidence gives `INDEPENDENCE_NOT_ESTABLISHED` and blocks.
12. **Reconciliation without voting.** Any evaluator disagreement, integrity defect, indeterminate mandatory predicate, missing discriminator, or specification-assurance failure blocks the applicable claim.
13. **Challenge-gate computation.** Recompute the challenge register and set `no_unresolved_blocking_challenge`; false makes `h=0`.
14. **Receipt freeze.** Bind every artifact, evidence class, proof, proficiency record, access reconciliation, challenge disposition, and reviewer/adjudication record.
15. **Controlled reveal and challenge window.** Release only predeclared details; accept challenges to any bound premise or artifact.
16. **Correction or closure.** Append disposition. Corrections create new versions and supersession edges; prior runs retain original hashes and standing.

An implementation output obtained before the prerequisite freezes cannot later be retrofitted into a valid sealed run.

## 5. Access-control model

### 5.1 Objects

- public benchmark specification;
- public fixture corpus;
- hidden input population;
- plaintext expectations;
- encrypted expectation bundles;
- keys and key shares;
- evaluator source/builds;
- implementation artifact and trace;
- reviewer rationales and conflict declarations;
- challenge evidence;
- append-only log and consistency proofs.

### 5.2 Access principles

1. **Least privilege:** access is object-, purpose-, version-, and time-scoped.
2. **No pre-freeze answer access:** implementation authors and submitters receive no plaintext expectation or hidden population.
3. **No adaptive evaluator query:** evaluators receive a frozen trace and scoped expectation material; they cannot ask the product to rerun after seeing hidden details.
4. **Two-person or threshold control for sensitive operations:** plaintext release, destructive key operations, and correction publication cannot be unilateral.
5. **Purpose binding:** a key or credential issued for evaluator comparison cannot authorize editing, challenge adjudication, or public release.
6. **Revocation with evidence preservation:** access can end without deleting the historical grant, read, denial, or revocation record.
7. **Conflict-aware grants:** a conflict declaration may restrict access; the restriction and rationale are logged.
8. **No “break glass” invisibility:** emergency access is possible only as a named, signed, reviewable event that invalidates or challenges affected runs unless the predeclared policy says otherwise.

### 5.3 Access event record

```yaml
OracleAccessEvent:
  event_id: opaque_identifier
  sequence_number: integer
  prior_log_head: digest
  occurred_at: timestamp
  actor_pseudonym: opaque_identifier
  role_assertion_digest: digest
  object_digest: digest
  object_class: stable_identifier
  action: grant | read | compare | deny | revoke | rotate | recover | reveal | destroy
  purpose_ref: stable_identifier
  evaluation_window_ref: opaque_identifier
  authorization_record_digest: digest
  result: completed | denied | failed
  environment_digest: digest | null
  emergency: boolean
  actor_signature: detached_signature
  witness_signatures: [detached_signature]
```

The oracle log records an attempt even when denied or failed. A missing event is not evidence that access did not occur. The receipt therefore cannot rely on `access_log_head` alone.

### 5.4 Independent access reconciliation

```yaml
AccessReconciliationRecord:
  evaluation_window_ref: opaque_identifier
  oracle_access_log_head: digest
  storage_audit_head: digest | null
  network_audit_head: digest | null
  key_service_audit_head: digest | null
  compared_event_window: interval
  matched_event_digests: [digest]
  missing_or_extra_event_digests: [digest]
  source_availability:
    oracle_log: available | unavailable
    storage_audit: available | unavailable
    network_audit: available | unavailable
    key_service_audit: available | unavailable
  disposition: consistent | inconsistent | not_established
  independent_reconciler_signature: detached_signature
```

- `consistent` is `independently_reconciled` evidence for the named window.
- `inconsistent` yields `RUN_INVALID` and a blocking challenge for every affected run.
- `not_established` yields `INDEPENDENCE_NOT_ESTABLISHED` and withholds the claim; it cannot be replaced by a custodian attestation.
- Emergency access, log gaps, and compromise windows remain preserved as evidence even after remediation.

`A-19` is the required falsifier: an omitted oracle event retained by storage/network/key evidence must make the run red.

## 6. Key lifecycle and rotation

1. Each expectation version is encrypted under a named cryptographic profile and key-generation event.
2. Key material is never stored in the same mutable object as the plaintext expectations or access log.
3. Rotation creates a new key version and a signed re-encryption or key-wrapping record; ciphertext and plaintext digests remain stable when semantics are unchanged.
4. Compromise response identifies the earliest possibly affected access window and challenges every run whose secrecy or authenticity depended on that key.
5. Expiry or personnel rotation must not make a prior receipt unverifiable. Verification keys, algorithm identifiers, certificates, and revocation evidence are preserved.
6. Destruction is permitted only when retention policy allows and after preserving enough evidence to verify prior commitments. Destruction never erases an access record.
7. Recovery is a sensitive event with threshold authorization and independent witnesses.
8. Algorithms and parameters are versioned. Migration creates a cross-commitment record binding old and new representations.

NIST SP 800-57 Part 1 Rev. 5 supplies lifecycle categories such as generation, protection, use, rotation, recovery, compromise, and destruction. It does not decide the final custody topology for PolicyOS.

## 7. Append-only log and commitment policy

The log contains at least:

- public-spec freeze entries;
- evaluator and generator build commitments;
- expectation leaves and Merkle roots;
- key generation/rotation/recovery/destruction events;
- access events;
- implementation submission freeze;
- hidden-population commitments;
- run starts/completions/failures;
- evaluator observations and signatures;
- reviewer conflicts, dissents, abstentions, and recusal;
- receipts;
- challenges and evidence digests;
- adjudication records;
- corrections and supersession edges;
- public disclosures and redactions.

Each entry binds the prior log head. Periodic signed tree heads and consistency proofs make equivocation detectable to independent witnesses. RFC 9162 is a transfer model for append-only Merkle consistency, not the final wire protocol.

## 8. Human review, proficiency, and adjudication protocol

### 8.1 Reviewer qualification and common-mode assurance

Before a reviewer population may support `S_v` for a named domain:

- eligibility, scope, conflicts, and prior roles are recorded;
- reviewers receive blinded proficiency anchors containing both product-side and specification-premise seeds;
- raw individual results, missed seeds, abstentions, and rationale digests are retained;
- a predeclared pass/renewal rule and drift schedule are applied without majority substitution;
- unanimity on live work has no positive force if the scope-relevant proficiency record is absent, stale, or failed.

```yaml
ReviewerQualificationRecord:
  reviewer_ref: opaque_identifier
  scope_profile_digest: digest
  blinded_anchor_set_digest: digest
  product_fault_results: [digest]
  specification_fault_results: [digest]
  missed_seed_digests: [digest]
  drift_check_digests: [digest]
  qualification_disposition: accepted_for_named_scope | not_established
  expires_or_review_due: timestamp_or_event_ref
  signature: detached_signature
```

`A-16` requires `SPECIFICATION_ASSURANCE_NOT_ESTABLISHED` when every otherwise competent reviewer misses a seeded shared premise. This preserves unanimity as evidence without treating it as truth.

### 8.2 Review packet

A reviewer receives the public axiom/fixture versions, independently derived expectation or predicate, rationale/forbidden-inference list, conflicts/prior roles, blinded product identity where feasible, and no unnecessary implementation source or pre-freeze hidden-run history.

### 8.3 Individual position

Each reviewer signs one of `supports`, `dissents`, `abstains`, or `conflict_recusal`. No position is silently converted. Abstention is neither negative nor affirmative; recusal remains in denominator reporting.

### 8.4 Disagreement handling

1. Assemble both evaluator observations, every signed position, exact artifact digests, proficiency scope, and minimal witnesses.
2. Classify the disagreement without erasing it: implementation, evaluator-R, evaluator-P, expectation, public axiom/specification, fixture ambiguity, observation insufficiency, integrity/access, competence, or unresolved.
3. Permit append-only clarifications.
4. Apply only a predeclared rule; no ad hoc majority vote.
5. If unresolved, withhold the affected claim and preserve `unresolved`.
6. Resolution produces a signed record and, when required, a new superseding artifact version.

### 8.5 Adjudication record

```yaml
AdjudicationRecord:
  adjudication_id: opaque_identifier
  challenged_object_digests: [digest]
  run_receipt_digest: digest | null
  issue_class: stable_identifier
  evidence_digests: [digest]
  reviewer_position_digests: [digest]
  reviewer_qualification_digests: [digest]
  conflict_and_recusal_digests: [digest]
  disposition: no_change | implementation_issue | evaluator_supersession |
               expectation_supersession | axiom_supersession |
               fixture_supersession | run_invalidated | unresolved
  rationale_digest: digest
  effective_scope: structured_scope
  superseding_object_digests: [digest]
  prior_run_effect: unchanged | challenged | invalidated_for_named_scope
  signatures: [detached_signature]
```

These are protocol observations, not a product status lattice.

## 9. Challenge protocol

### 9.1 Who may challenge

Implementation teams, evaluator teams, reviewers, public-interest observers, auditors, or other qualified parties may submit evidence-backed challenges. This research appoints none and fixes no final standing requirements.

### 9.2 Challenge classes and claim effect

A challenge is **blocking** for the named scope when it concerns:

- specification/axiom or expectation correctness;
- prohibited shared provenance, answer-neutrality, authorship, role conflict, or discriminator adequacy;
- expected-answer leakage or hidden-population compromise;
- evaluator, generator, relation-validator, mutation-certificate, or predicate-compiler validity;
- population/denominator suppression;
- access reconciliation, key compromise, equivocation, canonicalization, or oracle history;
- discarded dissent/abstention/recusal or unresolved evaluator disagreement; or
- nonreproducible bound artifacts/environment.

A challenge may be classified nonblocking only when its predeclared class cannot change any bound predicate or claim scope, and the classification itself is signed and challengeable. `no_unresolved_blocking_challenge` is recomputed over the complete challenge register and is a required conjunct of `h`.

### 9.3 Challenge stages

1. signed submission with target digests, class, scope, evidence, and requested remedy;
2. completeness/conflict triage, without merits decision by the challenged artifact's sole author;
3. preservation of affected logs, keys, artifacts, traces, and independent audit heads;
4. append-only response;
5. independent review under the adjudication protocol;
6. disposition preserving unresolved positions;
7. supersession where warranted; and
8. public notice to the permitted extent.

A receipt with any unresolved blocking challenge cannot render the `S0-K16` passage sentence (`A-21`). A challenge can invalidate or withhold a bounded claim; it cannot grant product authority or legal sufficiency.

## 10. Correction and supersession

### 10.1 Immutable binding rule

A run receipt binds:

```text
implementation revision
+ implementation artifact digest
+ environment/configuration digests
+ public specification and fixture-population digests
+ evaluator R/P versions and artifacts
+ generator version and seed commitment
+ expectation bundle version and commitment
+ access-log head
+ reviewer/adjudication record digests
```

Changing any bound element creates a different run identity. A corrected expectation cannot be substituted into an old receipt.

### 10.2 Correction sequence

1. Log the challenge or discovered defect against the old digest.
2. Preserve the old plaintext, ciphertext, salt, commitment proof, reviewer record, and access history under retention rules.
3. Produce a corrected artifact with a new version and digest.
4. Record `supersedes` and a reason; obtain required independent signatures.
5. Publish a new commitment before using the correction in a new evaluation.
6. Mark prior runs only through an appended effect record: `unchanged`, `challenged`, or `invalidated_for_named_scope`.
7. Never recompute an old score or receipt in place. Comparative re-evaluation, if authorized later, is a new run with a new receipt.

### 10.3 Silent-change falsifier

Given receipt `Q_old` bound to `O_v`, an attempt to present `O_v+1` as if it were used in `Q_old` must fail digest verification. Expected outcome: `RUN_INVALID` and `ORACLE_HISTORY_VIOLATION`; the old receipt and new supersession record remain available. If the system permits the substitution without detection, the oracle custody architecture is falsified.

## 11. External-regime transfers

| Regime | Transfer to custody benchmark | What does not transfer |
|---|---|---|
| ISO/IEC 17043:2023 and ILAC P9:01/2024 proficiency testing | Provider competence/impartiality, preplanned schemes, assigned/reference values, participant comparison, and treatment of anomalous results. | Software semantics are not physical measurands; no ISO accreditation is claimed; one benchmark run does not establish general competence. |
| ISO/IEC 17011:2017 and ISO/IEC 17025:2017 | Impartiality, competence, scope, consistent operation, and separation between accreditation and assessed work. | A repository team is not thereby an accreditation body or laboratory. |
| JCGM 200:2012 / ILAC P10:07/2020 | Traceability as a documented chain to stated references, with each link and uncertainty/limitation visible. | There is no SI-unit traceability for a normative custody outcome; the analogue is provenance traceability, not metrological equivalence. |
| GAO-24-106786 / NIST AC-5 | Independence, objectivity, quality management, documented role separation, and evidence of controls. | No government-audit opinion or security authorization follows. |
| Ofqual Condition G4 | Confidential material when secrecy is necessary for valid measurement; competent conflict-free investigation of breaches. | Candidates and software implementations are not identical, and secrecy alone does not make an assessment valid. |
| RFC 8785, RFC 9162, NIST SP 800-57 Pt.1 Rev.5 | Canonical commitments, append-only consistency proofs, key lifecycle, and verifiable corrections. | Cryptographic integrity does not prove semantic correctness. |

## 12. Standing

The amended protocol constructs the missing role, proficiency, access-reconciliation, specification-assurance, and challenge-gate predicates at the research-specification level. It still appoints no independent custodian, evaluator function, reviewer population, adjudication body, or log witness, and no operational evidence demonstrates that these gates exist. The standing remains `accepted_narrow_scope` because technical execution evidence and the second competent independent institution are both unestablished. No score, capability, owner appointment, or OPS-R15 unblock follows.
