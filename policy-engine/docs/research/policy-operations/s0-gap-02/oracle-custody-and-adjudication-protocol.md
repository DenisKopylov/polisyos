# Oracle custody, authority axioms, access, challenge, and adjudication

## 1. The oracle is itself a custody subject

A sealed expectation is not timeless truth. It is a versioned research artifact whose creation, access, interpretation, correction, and use must be attributable. The benchmark is therefore invalid if it applies stronger custody expectations to the implementation than to its own oracle.

The governing lens distinguishes facts, capability, authority, institutional acts, implementation state, and legal sufficiency. A benchmark may stipulate a synthetic scenario and test observable behavior; it may not convert that scenario into proof that a real authority or institution acted. (`policy-engine/docs/system-design-decisions/stage0-custody-kernel-ratification.md:46-88@1a7a2d05ebba22fae80e9934329e4b880806588e`, authority-band lens; `policy-engine/docs/system-design-decisions/stage0-custody-kernel-ratification.md:164-176@1a7a2d05ebba22fae80e9934329e4b880806588e`, application note and finding `S0-K15`.)

## 2. Authority-scenario axioms

### 2.1 Axiom record

Each authority premise is a versioned, challengeable record:

```yaml
AuthorityScenarioAxiom:
  axiom_id: stable_identifier
  version: semver_like_string
  proposition: declarative_expression
  scope:
    synthetic_jurisdiction_profile: stable_identifier
    subject_kinds: [stable_identifier]
    operation_kinds: [stable_identifier]
    temporal_scope: interval_or_rule
  standing: stipulated | contested | unknown
  provenance:
    source_refs: [stable_identifier]
    derivation_digest: digest
    authoring_record_digest: digest
  permitted_inferences: [stable_identifier]
  forbidden_inferences: [stable_identifier]
  dissent_position_refs: [opaque_handle]
  abstention_refs: [opaque_handle]
  supersedes_axiom_digest: digest | null
  may_not_use_for: [string]
```

### 2.2 Required axiom families

The benchmark profile should be able to express, without prescribing production internals:

- **scope axioms:** tenant, cell, matter, purpose, subject, operation, and time boundaries;
- **authority-source axioms:** what assertion exists in the synthetic scenario and its stipulated effective interval;
- **revocation/narrowing axioms:** how a stipulated authority-only change differs from a payload change;
- **dependency axioms:** which public semantic relationships are declared, not how product code traverses them;
- **observation axioms:** which channels are visible to evaluators and when;
- **historicity axioms:** distinctions among effective time, observation time, transaction time, and run time;
- **external-act axioms:** which attempted operations count as observable protected acts;
- **ambiguity axioms:** finite branches caused by contested or missing facts;
- **non-inference axioms:** explicit statements that signatures, possession, syntax validity, or historical publication do not alone establish current authority.

### 2.3 Axiom discipline

1. Axioms are synthetic premises for a test; they do not claim legal sufficiency.
2. Every permitted inference is paired with forbidden inferences so that an evaluator cannot silently broaden the premise.
3. Contested propositions retain signed positions. They are not normalized to `true` merely to make a fixture executable.
4. An `unknown` premise either creates a bounded alternative branch or makes the fixture non-claimable.
5. A change to an axiom creates a new digest and a supersession edge. It never rewrites the premise used by a prior run.
6. No axiom may be generated by observing current implementation behavior.
7. Reviewers may challenge the benchmark’s premise even when the implementation matches the sealed expectation.

## 3. Abstract roles and separation of duties

These are required role classes and incompatibilities, not appointments.

| Abstract role | Minimum function | Must not also perform for the same evaluation window |
|---|---|---|
| Scenario author | Draft public axioms and input-only fixtures. | Product implementation author; final expectation sealer acting alone; sole adjudicator of a challenge to their own axiom. |
| Expectation author | Derive explicit alternatives, exclusions, and predicates from public axioms. | Product implementation author; run submitter; sole key custodian; sole adjudicator of their own bundle. |
| Evaluator-R author/reviewer | Implement and review declarative reducer `R_v`. | Evaluator-P primary author; product semantic implementation author; plaintext expectation custodian before evaluator freeze. |
| Evaluator-P author/reviewer | Implement and review predicate/metamorphic evaluator `P_v`. | Evaluator-R primary author; product semantic implementation author; plaintext expectation custodian before evaluator freeze. |
| Commitment operator | Publish digests, Merkle roots, and signatures. | Unilateral plaintext editor; unilateral correction approver. |
| Key/access custodian | Control encrypted expectation access and key lifecycle. | Product submitter; sole evaluator; sole challenge adjudicator; unilateral log editor. |
| Run operator | Execute frozen artifacts and collect immutable traces. | Unilateral expectation editor; product revision mutator after freeze. |
| Challenger | Submit evidence-backed objections. | No blanket incompatibility; conflicts must be declared and visible. |
| Adjudication function | Classify a challenge and record disposition. | Sole author of the challenged axiom, expectation, or evaluator behavior; person with an undeclared material conflict. |
| Log witness/auditor | Verify append-only history, access events, and consistency proofs. | Sole log administrator or sole signing-key holder. |

NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 control `AC-5` supplies a general separation-of-duties pattern; GAO-24-106786 supplies public-audit independence and quality-management principles. The transfer is role incompatibility and evidence, not adoption of either regime as an accreditation decision.

## 4. Commitment and run sequence

The sequence is stateful and append-only:

1. **Public-spec freeze.** Publish `B` digest, axiom-profile digest, trace-observation profile, and corpus-manifest digest.
2. **Evaluator freeze.** Independently build `R_v`, `P_v`, and `M_v`; publish source/artifact/SBOM/provenance digests.
3. **Expectation freeze.** Canonicalize, salt, encrypt, and commit `O_v`; publish the Merkle root and detached signatures. Plaintext remains restricted.
4. **Implementation freeze.** Record implementation revision, artifact digest, SBOM, environment, configuration, and submission timestamp.
5. **Post-freeze population derivation.** Generate or select hidden adjacent fixtures under committed generator and seed rules; append their input commitments without exposing expectations.
6. **Execution.** Run `I`; capture an immutable raw observable trace. Run `C` separately as diagnostic. `C` cannot satisfy verification.
7. **Independent evaluation.** Run `R_v` and `P_v` in isolated environments. Record all reads, comparisons, findings, abstentions, and failures.
8. **Reconciliation without voting.** Compare evaluator observations. Any disagreement, integrity defect, or indeterminate mandatory predicate blocks the bounded verification claim pending challenge/adjudication.
9. **Receipt freeze.** Bind every digest, population, environment, evaluator version, expectation version, mutation seed commitment, access-log head, and signed reviewer/adjudication record.
10. **Controlled reveal.** Release only the expectation details and proofs authorized by the predeclared disclosure policy. Reveal must not change the receipt.
11. **Challenge window.** Accept challenges to fixtures, axioms, expectations, evaluators, access, cryptography, mutations, or implementation observations.
12. **Correction or closure.** Append disposition. Corrections create new versions and supersession edges; prior runs retain their original standing and hashes.

An implementation output obtained before steps 1–4 are complete cannot later be retrofitted into a valid sealed run, because the oracle could have been adapted to the answer.

## 5. Access-control model

### 5.1 Objects

- public benchmark specification;
- public fixture corpus;
- hidden input population;
- plaintext expectations;
- encrypted expectation bundles;
- keys and key shares;
- evaluator source/builds;
- implementation artifact and trace;
- reviewer rationales and conflict declarations;
- challenge evidence;
- append-only log and consistency proofs.

### 5.2 Access principles

1. **Least privilege:** access is object-, purpose-, version-, and time-scoped.
2. **No pre-freeze answer access:** implementation authors and submitters receive no plaintext expectation or hidden population.
3. **No adaptive evaluator query:** evaluators receive a frozen trace and scoped expectation material; they cannot ask the product to rerun after seeing hidden details.
4. **Two-person or threshold control for sensitive operations:** plaintext release, destructive key operations, and correction publication cannot be unilateral.
5. **Purpose binding:** a key or credential issued for evaluator comparison cannot authorize editing, challenge adjudication, or public release.
6. **Revocation with evidence preservation:** access can end without deleting the historical grant, read, denial, or revocation record.
7. **Conflict-aware grants:** a conflict declaration may restrict access; the restriction and rationale are logged.
8. **No “break glass” invisibility:** emergency access is possible only as a named, signed, reviewable event that invalidates or challenges affected runs unless the predeclared policy says otherwise.

### 5.3 Access event record

```yaml
OracleAccessEvent:
  event_id: opaque_identifier
  sequence_number: integer
  prior_log_head: digest
  occurred_at: timestamp
  actor_pseudonym: opaque_identifier
  role_assertion_digest: digest
  object_digest: digest
  object_class: stable_identifier
  action: grant | read | compare | deny | revoke | rotate | recover | reveal | destroy
  purpose_ref: stable_identifier
  evaluation_window_ref: opaque_identifier
  authorization_record_digest: digest
  result: completed | denied | failed
  environment_digest: digest | null
  emergency: boolean
  actor_signature: detached_signature
  witness_signatures: [detached_signature]
```

The log records an access attempt even when denied or failed. A missing event is not evidence that access did not occur, so system architecture must also produce independent storage, network, and key-service audit evidence.

## 6. Key lifecycle and rotation

1. Each expectation version is encrypted under a named cryptographic profile and key-generation event.
2. Key material is never stored in the same mutable object as the plaintext expectations or access log.
3. Rotation creates a new key version and a signed re-encryption or key-wrapping record; ciphertext and plaintext digests remain stable when semantics are unchanged.
4. Compromise response identifies the earliest possibly affected access window and challenges every run whose secrecy or authenticity depended on that key.
5. Expiry or personnel rotation must not make a prior receipt unverifiable. Verification keys, algorithm identifiers, certificates, and revocation evidence are preserved.
6. Destruction is permitted only when retention policy allows and after preserving enough evidence to verify prior commitments. Destruction never erases an access record.
7. Recovery is a sensitive event with threshold authorization and independent witnesses.
8. Algorithms and parameters are versioned. Migration creates a cross-commitment record binding old and new representations.

NIST SP 800-57 Part 1 Rev. 5 supplies lifecycle categories such as generation, protection, use, rotation, recovery, compromise, and destruction. It does not decide the final custody topology for PolicyOS.

## 7. Append-only log and commitment policy

The log contains at least:

- public-spec freeze entries;
- evaluator and generator build commitments;
- expectation leaves and Merkle roots;
- key generation/rotation/recovery/destruction events;
- access events;
- implementation submission freeze;
- hidden-population commitments;
- run starts/completions/failures;
- evaluator observations and signatures;
- reviewer conflicts, dissents, abstentions, and recusal;
- receipts;
- challenges and evidence digests;
- adjudication records;
- corrections and supersession edges;
- public disclosures and redactions.

Each entry binds the prior log head. Periodic signed tree heads and consistency proofs make equivocation detectable to independent witnesses. RFC 9162 is a transfer model for append-only Merkle consistency, not the final wire protocol.

## 8. Human review and adjudication protocol

### 8.1 Review packet

A reviewer receives:

- the public axiom and fixture versions;
- the independently derived expectation alternative or predicate under review;
- a structured rationale and forbidden-inference list;
- conflicts and prior roles;
- blinded product identity where feasible;
- no unnecessary implementation source or pre-freeze hidden-run history.

### 8.2 Individual position

Each reviewer signs one of:

- `supports` — agrees that the named alternative/predicate follows from the stated axiom within scope;
- `dissents` — identifies a different bounded alternative, exclusion, axiom interpretation, or non-claimability reason;
- `abstains` — lacks competence, evidence, or confidence to take a position;
- `conflict_recusal` — has a conflict that prevents participation.

No position is silently converted. Abstention is not a negative vote and not an affirmative vote. Recusal does not disappear from denominator reporting.

### 8.3 Disagreement handling

1. Assemble a disagreement packet containing both evaluators’ observations, all signed positions, the exact fixture/axiom/expectation/evaluator digests, and minimal witnesses.
2. Classify the disagreement without erasing it:
   - implementation trace defect;
   - evaluator-R defect;
   - evaluator-P defect;
   - expectation defect;
   - public axiom/specification defect;
   - fixture ambiguity not represented;
   - observation-interface insufficiency;
   - integrity/access defect;
   - competence shortfall;
   - unresolved.
3. Permit each position holder to append clarification; prior text remains immutable.
4. Apply only a predeclared adjudication rule. No ad hoc majority vote is allowed after seeing the desired result.
5. If the classification remains unresolved, withhold the bounded verification claim for the affected scope and record `unresolved`; do not manufacture a single truth label.
6. A resolution produces a signed adjudication record and, where needed, a new superseding artifact version. It never edits the historical position set.

### 8.4 Adjudication record

```yaml
AdjudicationRecord:
  adjudication_id: opaque_identifier
  challenged_object_digests: [digest]
  run_receipt_digest: digest | null
  issue_class: stable_identifier
  evidence_digests: [digest]
  reviewer_position_digests: [digest]
  conflict_and_recusal_digests: [digest]
  disposition: no_change | implementation_issue | evaluator_supersession |
               expectation_supersession | axiom_supersession |
               fixture_supersession | run_invalidated | unresolved
  rationale_digest: digest
  effective_scope: structured_scope
  superseding_object_digests: [digest]
  prior_run_effect: unchanged | challenged | invalidated_for_named_scope
  signatures: [detached_signature]
```

These dispositions are protocol observations, not a product status lattice.

## 9. Challenge protocol

### 9.1 Who may challenge

The design permits challenges from implementation teams, evaluator teams, reviewers, affected public-interest observers, auditors, or other qualified parties. This research appoints none of them and sets no final standing requirements.

### 9.2 Challenge grounds

- expected-answer leakage or memorization opportunity;
- prohibited shared code, generated artifact, network service, authoring input, or dependency;
- incorrect public axiom or forbidden legal inference;
- unbounded or catch-all ambiguity;
- omitted admissible alternative or exclusion;
- evaluator defect or predicate incompleteness;
- mutation that is not semantics-preserving or not adjacent as declared;
- access-log gap, unauthorized read, key compromise, or equivocation;
- discarded dissent, abstention, recusal, or evaluator disagreement;
- canonicalization/parser differential;
- nonreproducible run environment or receipt;
- selective fixture suppression or post-output oracle adaptation.

### 9.3 Challenge stages

1. **Submission:** signed claim, target digests, scope, evidence, requested remedy.
2. **Triage:** completeness and conflict review; no merits decision by the challenged artifact’s sole author.
3. **Preservation:** freeze affected logs, keys, artifacts, and traces; publish a challenge commitment.
4. **Response:** artifact authors may append evidence and rationale.
5. **Independent review:** apply the adjudication protocol.
6. **Disposition:** append record; preserve unresolved positions.
7. **Supersession:** create new versions when warranted.
8. **Public notice:** disclose the existence and effect of the challenge to the extent the disclosure policy permits without leaking live expectations.

A challenge can invalidate a run’s bounded verification claim. It cannot grant product authority or legal sufficiency.

## 10. Correction and supersession

### 10.1 Immutable binding rule

A run receipt binds:

```text
implementation revision
+ implementation artifact digest
+ environment/configuration digests
+ public specification and fixture-population digests
+ evaluator R/P versions and artifacts
+ generator version and seed commitment
+ expectation bundle version and commitment
+ access-log head
+ reviewer/adjudication record digests
```

Changing any bound element creates a different run identity. A corrected expectation cannot be substituted into an old receipt.

### 10.2 Correction sequence

1. Log the challenge or discovered defect against the old digest.
2. Preserve the old plaintext, ciphertext, salt, commitment proof, reviewer record, and access history under retention rules.
3. Produce a corrected artifact with a new version and digest.
4. Record `supersedes` and a reason; obtain required independent signatures.
5. Publish a new commitment before using the correction in a new evaluation.
6. Mark prior runs only through an appended effect record: `unchanged`, `challenged`, or `invalidated_for_named_scope`.
7. Never recompute an old score or receipt in place. Comparative re-evaluation, if authorized later, is a new run with a new receipt.

### 10.3 Silent-change falsifier

Given receipt `Q_old` bound to `O_v`, an attempt to present `O_v+1` as if it were used in `Q_old` must fail digest verification. Expected outcome: `RUN_INVALID` and `ORACLE_HISTORY_VIOLATION`; the old receipt and new supersession record remain available. If the system permits the substitution without detection, the oracle custody architecture is falsified.

## 11. External-regime transfers

| Regime | Transfer to custody benchmark | What does not transfer |
|---|---|---|
| ISO/IEC 17043:2023 and ILAC P9:01/2024 proficiency testing | Provider competence/impartiality, preplanned schemes, assigned/reference values, participant comparison, and treatment of anomalous results. | Software semantics are not physical measurands; no ISO accreditation is claimed; one benchmark run does not establish general competence. |
| ISO/IEC 17011:2017 and ISO/IEC 17025:2017 | Impartiality, competence, scope, consistent operation, and separation between accreditation and assessed work. | A repository team is not thereby an accreditation body or laboratory. |
| JCGM 200:2012 / ILAC P10:07/2020 | Traceability as a documented chain to stated references, with each link and uncertainty/limitation visible. | There is no SI-unit traceability for a normative custody outcome; the analogue is provenance traceability, not metrological equivalence. |
| GAO-24-106786 / NIST AC-5 | Independence, objectivity, quality management, documented role separation, and evidence of controls. | No government-audit opinion or security authorization follows. |
| Ofqual Condition G4 | Confidential material when secrecy is necessary for valid measurement; competent conflict-free investigation of breaches. | Candidates and software implementations are not identical, and secrecy alone does not make an assessment valid. |
| RFC 8785, RFC 9162, NIST SP 800-57 Pt.1 Rev.5 | Canonical commitments, append-only consistency proofs, key lifecycle, and verifiable corrections. | Cryptographic integrity does not prove semantic correctness. |

## 12. Standing

The protocol is architecturally coherent but institutionally conditional. No independent custodian, competent dual-evaluator function, adjudication body, or log witness is appointed or evidenced by this research. That open institutional dependency is a principal reason for `accepted_narrow_scope`.
