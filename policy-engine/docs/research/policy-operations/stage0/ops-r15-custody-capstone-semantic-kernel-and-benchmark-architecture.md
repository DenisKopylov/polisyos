# OPS-R15 — Custody-Capstone Semantic Kernel and Benchmark Architecture

## Executive Finding

**Result: `blocked_pending_oracle_independence`.**

The principal custody proposition is strong: a long-lived PolicyOS custody
implementation must preserve durable subject-bound suspension, exact wake
matching, action-specific authority re-admission, content-versus-authority
invalidation, append-only correction, cutoff replay, scoped impact, tenant and
jurisdiction isolation, external-act separation, public currentness, and
recoverable custody state.

The delivered 24-month Markdown calendar is a scenario catalogue, not an
independent executable benchmark. Inputs and expected results are visible
together; event names do not normalize to the declared vocabulary; no
machine-readable corpus, runner, sealed semantic oracle, independent evaluator,
access protocol, or exercised anti-leakage process exists. A clean rebuild may
share the same faulty reducers and dependency graph.

The corrected Stage-0 contribution is a set of implementation-neutral semantic
predicates and task-owned conformance profiles. State names, a universal event
envelope, thirteen clocks, twenty universal gates, two physical graphs, exact
`WorldRelease` structure, institutional authority outcomes, efficiency
thresholds, and RPO/RTO numbers are not Stage-0 contracts. OPS-R15 remains
blocked until
[S0-GAP-02](../consolidation/stage0/stage0-additional-research-register.md#s0-gap-02)
produces an independent oracle/evaluator architecture and benchmark
engineering makes it executable.

The exact source is preserved at
[sources/ops-r15-original.md](sources/ops-r15-original.md). Its embedded
conversation citation markers remain part of the frozen historical bytes; no
such markers are used as evidence in this revision.

# 1. Task And Project Fit

## 1.1 Narrow research question

What implementation-neutral, adversarial, and independently evaluable
properties would demonstrate that PolicyOS kept its own justification honest
through a long custody cycle without silently executing external
administrative functions?

This revision answers only the semantic-kernel and benchmark-architecture
portion. It does not claim that the benchmark exists or has passed.

## 1.2 Boundary verdict

PolicyOS **OWNS** custody of its signed claims, benchmark input/run artifacts
that it produces, and honest bounded reporting. External legal,
administrative, service, payment, records, and oversight events remain
externally owned; their evidence relationships may be **INTEGRATE**. Contextual
signals may be **OBSERVE**. Execution of PolicyOS anti-roles is
**OUT_OF_SCOPE**.

The benchmark has no authority to change those verdicts.

## 1.3 Bounded pass claim

A future pass may state only:

> The named implementation and repository revision, in the recorded
> environment, satisfied the committed predicates for the closed fixture
> population, declared scenario axioms, and evaluator version.

A pass does not establish legal compliance, institutional competence,
production resilience, production readiness, or authority to perform an
external act.

## 1.4 Relationship to Stage-0 and Wave 2

- PAO-R0 permits fixture-local opaque subjects but leaves their canonical owner
  and ABI open.
- PAO-R1 supplies the boundary-plane method; the 213-row census is not oracle
  truth.
- OPS-R1/OPS-R3 own suspension/resume and migration research.
- OPS-R2 owns authority dependencies and affected-set semantics.
- OPS-R4 owns production temporal vocabulary and correction algebra.
- OPS-R8 owns `WorldRelease` research.
- INT-R5 owns competence/delegation research for protected actions.
- PAO-R36, INT-R7/INT-R8, and Atlas own public correction and verification
  questions.
- OPS-R14 owns deployment-specific resilience and recovery evidence.

OPS-R15 tests accepted outputs from these owners. It must not become their
contract owner.

## 4.2 Action-specific protection

The source report's twenty gates are not one universal resume transaction.
Equivalent protection is required at the phase where the relevant risk can
materialize:

| Protection class | Required property |
|---|---|
| Core reconstruction | Durable state, integrity, exact subject/scope binding, idempotence |
| Conditional evidence | Freshness, compatibility, source/authority validity when depended upon |
| Action-specific authority | Authorization, purpose, tenant, jurisdiction, competence, permitted use |
| Human conditions | Required mandate, delegation, conflict, or review conditions for that action |
| Pre-publication | Currentness, correction/supersession, audience and redaction constraints |
| Asynchronous follow-up | Impact fan-out, acquisition, revalidation, correction, audit preservation |

A correct implementation may distribute these checks while preserving the
observable property.

## 4.3 Content and authority dependencies

The benchmark must distinguish:

- a **content dependency**, where payload or derived values changed; and
- an **authority dependency**, where unchanged content lost admissibility or
  permitted use.

This is a semantic distinction, not a requirement for exactly two physical
graphs or five exhaustive impact sets. OPS-R2 owns representation, overlap,
affected-set closure, and pruning. The future evaluator observes whether every
independently expected dependent claim reacted and every non-dependent claim
remained unchanged.

## 4.4 Temporal roles without a universal envelope

Fixtures must preserve source occurrence/effect, PolicyOS custody/admission,
transaction/history, and correction/replay roles when material. Domain owners
choose their family clocks; OPS-R4 defines the production algebra. Evaluator
delivery and storage times are benchmark controls and never content authority.

No thirteen-field event header is required.

## 4.5 Scenario catalogue standing

The original 24-month calendar is retained only as a source catalogue for
future input-only fixtures. Its principal scenario strata are:

- suspension, dormant custody, wake, duplicate, and look-alike events;
- legal amendment, repeal, retroactivity, jurisdiction, and competence;
- data/schema/model/metric/license/contract changes;
- KPI observation, incident, evaluation, and learning;
- appeal, notice, remedy, payment, service, procurement, and records
  boundary traps;
- public correction, supersession, withdrawal, key rotation, and archival
  verification;
- partial failure, asymmetric recovery, stale caches, and clean rebuild;
- matter continuity/split/merge hypotheses;
- fleet, world-release, multilingual, and resilience extensions.

The last four categories must be allocated to task-owned extension packs where
their semantic owners and scenario assumptions can be reviewed.

## 4.6 External and human facts

Synthetic institutional outcomes are **scenario axioms**, not legal ground
truth. A fixture must state:

- jurisdiction and applicable period;
- asserted external operator and competence basis;
- which facts are fixed versus contested;
- the source and version of the axiom;
- raw reviewer labels, abstentions, disagreements, and corrections;
- the permitted claim scope.

An oracle correction appends a new version and never silently changes the
record of a prior scored run.

# 6. Benchmark Or Fixture Proposal

## 6.1 Four-package architecture

An executable benchmark requires four separately governed packages:

| Package | Visibility and contents | Prohibited contents |
|---|---|---|
| Public specification | Predicates, schemas, equivalence policy, bounded claim, governance rules | Hidden expected results |
| Implementation-visible input | Input-only fixtures, opaque IDs, family discriminator, scoped source refs, delivery controls | Expected wakes, impacts, reactions, public posture, oracle labels |
| Sealed expectation/oracle | Versioned accepted predicates, admissible alternatives, authority scenario axioms, reviewer evidence | Implementation code or mutable post-run edits |
| Evaluator/run package | Independent evaluator, environment/version refs, commitments, access log, immutable result and challenge links | Shared semantic reducers or undocumented exclusions |

The package commitments must be recorded before scoring. Access, rotation,
challenge, correction, and retirement require an exercised governance
protocol, not prose saying a set is hidden.

## 6.2 Independence requirement

A full rebuild using implementation admission code, reducers, dependency
traversal, or status projection is a consistency check. Semantic correctness
requires an independently owned declarative evaluator with an explicit
equivalence relation and no shared semantic implementation.

At minimum, S0-GAP-02 must decide:

- evaluator ownership and code-independence rules;
- accepted versus prohibited shared dependencies;
- representation of ambiguity and multiple acceptable outcomes;
- sealing and commitment;
- reviewer competence, conflicts, dissent, and correction;
- adjacent-case and metamorphic mutation generation;
- reproducibility and challenge receipts.

## 6.3 Mutation and anti-overfitting controls

Future scoring must include:

1. opaque ID renumbering;
2. permitted event-order permutations;
3. duplicates and replay;
4. wrong-tenant, wrong-subject, wrong-jurisdiction, and wrong-purpose
   look-alikes;
5. malformed, unsigned, revoked, stale, and contradictory evidence;
6. authority-only invalidation with identical payload bytes;
7. late and retroactive correction;
8. at least one adjacent unseen case;
9. seeded shared-reducer faults used to verify evaluator independence;
10. controlled-surface cache and projection variants.

Failed runs and all oracle/evaluator versions remain auditable. No fixture,
threshold, or denominator may be changed after observing a result without
creating a new benchmark version.

## 6.4 Metrics

### Critical predicates

Critical semantic metrics are evaluated over a closed fixture population with
independently established expected results:

```text
critical_pass =
  every applicable fixture predicate is satisfied
  and every prohibited external action count is zero
```

The report sets no global production zero outside the declared closed
population.

### Affected-set measures

Recall and precision are meaningful only after an independent oracle defines
the complete affected and unaffected sets:

```text
affected_recall =
  correctly_reacted_expected_dependencies / all_expected_dependencies

affected_precision =
  correctly_reacted_expected_dependencies / all_reacted_dependencies
```

Ambiguous or disputed items must remain visible and follow the declared scoring
policy; they cannot be silently removed from denominators.

### Diagnostic measures

Reuse, minimal recomputation, escalation, latency, storage cost, recovery time,
and operator workload are diagnostic until their environment, denominator,
workload, and evidence basis are fixed. Stage 0 sets no arbitrary threshold and
no production RPO or RTO.

## 6.5 Result reporting

A run receipt must identify:

- repository and implementation commit;
- environment and dependency versions;
- public/input/oracle/evaluator package commitments;
- exact fixture population and mutations;
- raw predicate outcomes;
- ambiguity/disagreement handling;
- failed and excluded cases with pre-registered reasons;
- challenge/correction/supersession links;
- bounded pass, fail, blocked, or invalid-run conclusion.

The conclusion is benchmark-local and cannot become claim authority.

# 9. Promotion And Kill Rules

## 9.1 Research-only

Current standing. The semantic kernel and scenario catalogue can inform
research, but no scoring or pass claim is allowed.

## 9.2 Prototype allowed

An unscored prototype may build machine-readable input fixtures or evaluator
spikes if:

- outputs are labelled fixture-only and non-authoritative;
- input and expected packages are separated;
- no external act is executed;
- fixture-local subject references carry no canonical meaning;
- same-code rebuild is labelled diagnostic;
- failed runs and reviewer disagreement are preserved.

## 9.3 Governed benchmark candidate

Requires:

1. accepted S0-GAP-02 architecture;
2. machine-readable public/input/oracle/evaluator packages;
3. independent semantic implementation;
4. commitments, access logs, rotation, challenge, and correction;
5. closed denominators and ambiguity policy;
6. domain-owner review of predicates and scenario axioms;
7. adjacent unseen and metamorphic cases;
8. immutable failed-run evidence;
9. reproducible runner and bounded claim template.

## 9.4 Production-like evaluation

Additionally requires a representative topology, controlled surfaces,
partner/jurisdiction evidence, exercised recovery and key/archive procedures,
deployment-specific SLOs, and an explicit statement that benchmark passage is
not legal or institutional authority.

## 9.5 Mandatory block/kill rules

Block scoring or invalidate a run if:

- implementation-visible files reveal expected actions, labels, or public
  postures;
- evaluator semantics share implementation admission, reducers, dependency
  traversal, or status projection;
- fixture/oracle/evaluator commitments were not fixed before the run;
- an expected result was edited or a failed fixture excluded after observation;
- raw reviewer dissent or an admissible alternative was discarded;
- an external act is expected to be performed by PolicyOS;
- wrong-scope evidence, missing decisive evidence, or a projection upgrades
  authority;
- the corpus lacks ID/order/look-alike/adjacent-case mutations;
- metrics use open or changing denominators;
- a same-code rebuild is reported as independent semantic proof;
- a production RPO/RTO or readiness claim is inferred from synthetic time.

## Pattern pass

- **P01 — Contract-only capability:** prose, contracts, and local mechanics do
  not become a benchmark capability.
- **P04 — Status enum proliferation:** runtime state names are replaced by
  observable predicates.
- **P05 — Authority dilution:** a benchmark verdict, projection, wake, or
  receipt grants no authority.
- **P08 — Time semantics fragmentation:** family roles are preserved while
  OPS-R4 owns the algebra.
- **P10 — Structural-only validation:** schema and same-code parity cannot
  prove semantic adequacy.
- **P13 — Contract gravity well:** the benchmark does not dictate H2's event
  envelope, state machine, graph topology, or twenty-gate transaction.
- **P27 — Parallel implementation/canonical-owner bypass:** domain extensions
  remain task-owned.
- **P29 — Authorial proof/self-attested artifact:** visible, self-authored
  expected traces are rejected as executable proof.
- **P31 — Instance-patching over structural invariant:** predicates apply
  across all fixture families and controlled surfaces.
- **P32 — Trust-by-form:** event names, signatures, fields, and fixture status
  do not substitute for scope and authority verification.
- **P33 — Witness-as-spec:** IDs, order, look-alikes, malformed inputs, and
  adjacent cases are mutated.
- **P34 — Premature green via uncompleted exclusion:** failed fixtures remain
  scored unless a pre-registered, independently verified invalid-run rule
  applies.
