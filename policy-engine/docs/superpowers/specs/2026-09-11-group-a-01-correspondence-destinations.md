used to justify PolicyOS's own claim is therefore a reading the cited norms do
not eliminate. Whether that reading describes CORR-B1 is the missing application
of the boundary test. The unconditional “never” is not established by those two
quotations alone.

CORR-B2's source asks for a **typed semantic conversion of independent source
claims**, including estimand interpretation and producer independence from the
binding claim. Its title's word “external” does not by itself require the software
performing that conversion to be outside PolicyOS. External observation authorship
and internal typed extraction are different planes. The row's inference would
need independence to mean organizational externality, rather than independence
of the evidence from the proposition being tested.

The W5 enumeration supports **no explicit identity of appointment purposes** with
CORR-I2/I3. It does not prove no existing appointer, applicability resolver or
relation procedure could participate after a purpose-specific extension. In
particular, W5-R5-Q04's applicability resolver is more directly adjacent than the
two routes singled out in the ruling. Point (vi) provides general qualifications;
it does not establish the availability or demonstrated dual competence of a
holder, nor a complete purpose-specific assessment instrument.

**Agreement:** exact norm wording and separation of purposes agree. Permanent
external ownership, exhaustive non-overlap and discharge of a concrete dual
qualification are stronger claims than the evidence inspected here establishes.
No disposition or status is assigned by this report.

### The two ratified quotations, and what they actually bind

From `policy-engine/docs/system-design-decisions/policyos-identity-and-custody-boundary.md@63bdb9640`,
**§4, Anti-roles (binding)**:

> PolicyOS is not an administrator, not an executor, not a case-management system,
> not a court, not a notification channel, not a payment system, not a CRM. These
> are commodity or sovereign functions owned by other systems and institutions.

From the same document, **§9 item 6**, principal's 2026-08-31 ruling:

> an appointment binds the act it names, never the vocabulary that describes it.
> Naming an owner for a decision confers authority over producing the decision;
> it confers nothing over the types, protocols and frozen contracts by which that
> decision is spoken about, nor over a pure predicate that verifies an
> already-produced artifact is intact. Verifying a signature is not signing.

The same item defines its operational boundary:

> Everything that mints — produces a receipt, admits a pack, issues a certificate —
> stays with the appointed owner. Everything that only describes or checks may move.

Its worked owner is GY-O0, inside PolicyOS. Thus “minting requires the appropriate
owner” and “PolicyOS may never own this particular minting act” are not synonyms.
The latter requires classification of the particular act under **§5**. The file's
frontmatter excludes `jurisdiction_specific_legal_conclusion`; it cannot supply a
particular jurisdiction's legal determination by implication.

Countervailing ratified applications in **§6** are “Weekly legal release,
jurisdiction packs — OWN” and “PolicyMatter identity above a single case — OWN,
now.” These do **not** themselves authorize CORR-B1. They show why legal vocabulary
or identity creation is not categorically a sovereign adjudication. **S0-K03** in
`policy-engine/docs/system-design-decisions/stage0-custody-kernel-ratification.md@63bdb9640`
requires splitting the external act, evidence emission, admission, claim reaction
and projection before applying the boundary rule. **W5-K07** in
`policy-engine/docs/system-design-decisions/wave5-evidence-substitution-ratification.md@63bdb9640`
forbids substituting a statute's existence for its governing the lever; it supplies
no blanket allocation of every semantic identity producer to an external court.

## 4. What the architect's decision would rest on

The decisive fact for B1 is **which act is meant by minting**: sovereign determination
of a legal subject, or internal evidence-grounded canonicalization for PolicyOS's
own claims. Apply identity **§5/§6**, with the planes separated by **S0-K03**;
**§9 item 6** then distinguishes its vocabulary from its producer. Neither the key's
name nor “not a court” alone supplies that application. For B2 the corresponding
fact is whether the producer authors empirical truth or converts independently
produced source evidence with a check independent of the binding assertion.

For I2/I3, an equivalence claim would require the same exact substantive predicate,
competence, referent, scope and authority purpose in a W5 route. The eighteen texts
do not state that equality. For I1, the settling clarification is whether the
owed object is general qualification criteria, an operational two-profile
assessment contract, or a demonstrated holder; WP08 and RT11 speak to the latter,
while point (vi) expressly supplies only criteria.

Pattern pass: P05/P15 prohibit self-produced semantic truth acquiring authority;
P35/P36 require the complete routing denominator and exact ratified clauses;
P37/P38 distinguish an independently established predicate from a role/title or a
namespace label. This report neither creates a contract nor changes a register.
